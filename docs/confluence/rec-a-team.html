20. The agent team: configured, approved, self-healing, self-learning (phase 18, later)

Arun, 24 Oct 2026 (MA-127): "Keep everything configurable which I can control by Admin Web portal or telegram bots (only for admin) talking to Agents. The Agents should be able to ask me what is needed and ask for my approval for any kind of changes ... recalibrate and tuning you algorithms on daily basis ... This should be self healing and self learning system ... For any kind of issues, connect with the admin for approval ... This app is just for my household."

Three rules hold for every agent. Code decides, Claude explains: models and rules produce every number and decision; Claude tags, writes, asks and answers. Nothing changes without a trace: every behaviour is a setting, and every change is a version and an audit row. Outside its band, it asks: an agent may act on its own only inside the automatic band its settings allow; anything else is a request to the admin.

Each agent is a Lambda with its own role, tools, model routing and budget share. They do not call each other directly: they publish events on the mypaisa-<env>-events EventBridge bus and leave work in DynamoDB, and the daily pipeline is a Step Functions state machine (§20.5). No agent can trade, read a secret, delete data or apply infrastructure: secrets are put in SSM by hand, and infrastructure changes only through Terraform and CI (.claude/rules/aws-changes.md); an agent can at most open a Jira issue proposing one.

20.2 Settings: one store for every behaviour (E1, MA-129)

Every behaviour the household might want to change is a Setting: the data source chains, rule thresholds and model parameters, horizons, schedules, alert budgets, ceilings and agent shares, and the learning loop's own bounds. Each has a schema with an allowed range, an automatic band (how far an agent may move it without asking; often zero), and an approval rule. Defaults live in config/defaults.yaml, validated in CI; the live values in DynamoDB, one ConfigVersion row per change, never edited, with a daily snapshot to S3. One settings service makes every change and enforces the schema, the range and the approval rule, so no agent can write a setting directly. Lambdas read settings through a cache of a minute at most. Secrets stay in SSM and infrastructure in Terraform: a setting may name a secret, never hold one.

20.3 The admin desk: the owner's chat and the Admin center, the admin only (E2, MA-130)

The admin is the owner, the household's one admin (Arun, 25 Oct 2026: "there is only 1 admin"); members and viewers never see the desk. Both doors show the same queue:

  • In the chat, in the admin's own conversation with MyPaisa, and by push. A request arrives as a card, for example:
Data steward asks
yfinance has not returned Balanced for 3 days; Twelve Data does not list it.
EODHD's free key returns its history back to 2019.
Switch on EODHD Historian at £19.99 a month?
AWS and others: £3 of £50 used this month.
Undo: switch it off; nothing else changes.
[Approve] [Reject] [Why?] [Later]
  • In the Admin center, its Approvals, Settings, Agents and Incidents screens (designed first).

Approving needs the second check (§4.5, MA-76): a fresh Sign in with Apple or passkey under Face ID, or for a member who signs in only with Google, Google again. Deciding in one place updates the other. Each request says what changes, from and to, why with its evidence, the cost and the budget left, the risk and the undo, and when it expires. Agents also ask plain questions ("Which fund is the Monzo Balanced fund's ISIN?") with buttons or a typed answer that resumes their work. The admin gives instructions in their own words ("put Twelve Data first for London", "lower dev to $30", "why did the Forecaster go quiet on silver?"); the concierge turns an instruction into the same request with a Confirm card, and a question into an answer from the agent concerned. Non-urgent requests wait for one daily digest; incidents come at once; a rejected request is not proposed again for the period its setting names. Every decision is audited.

20.4 The learning loop: grade daily, calibrate daily, change rules on evidence (E4 to E6, MA-132 to MA-134)

Every prediction is kept exactly as it was made, with the versions of its model and settings and the date its inputs were known, and graded against what prices actually did:

  1. Grade, every day. After the close, each prediction whose horizon has passed gets its outcome: the realised return, hit or miss, Brier score, log loss, whether it fell inside its range. A late price correction adds a regraded outcome; nothing is overwritten. ScoreDaily keeps the scoreboard per model, holding and market mood, rolling over 30, 90 and 365 days.
  2. Calibrate, every day, within bounds. The calibration map of the rules engine and of each Forecaster model is refitted on a rolling window. A new map is used only if it beats the current one on the held-back part of the window, only where a bucket has enough graded cases, and no bucket moves more than two points in a day. It then serves a probation of 10 trading days and is rolled back on its own if it does worse.
  3. Change rules and models on evidence, with approval. Challengers (the monthly retrain, other parameters, rule thresholds within their ranges, new patterns and features, and heavier model families such as sequence models over the candles and indicators or a wider ensemble) are tested over ten years and run live in shadow. That is how the Forecaster grows (Arun, 29 Oct 2026: "self learning and self growing over time"): a new model earns its place by its record, never by being newer. One that beats the current version in the held-out years and over 8 live weeks, by more than a margin, is proposed on the admin desk with the scores side by side. After a promotion the old version stays ready and is restored on its own if the new one slips.
  4. Report. A line in the admin's daily digest, the scoreboard in the Saturday review, and a monthly note on what changed and why.

Why not retune the rules themselves every day: a day's outcomes are mostly noise, and a system that chases yesterday learns noise. Calibration moves a little every day; structure moves only on weeks of evidence. That is how accuracy and, above all, honest confidence grow over the years.

Grading runs daily because its ground truth, the day's prices, is new every day; it scores outcomes as they arrive. It is not an offline eval rerun on a timer, which §23 rules out (§23.6).

20.5 Self-healing (E7, MA-135)

The daily run (ingest, engine, Forecaster, grade, calibrate, brief) is one Step Functions state machine with retries and backoff on every step and idempotent writes, its jobs carried by the queues of §21. When a dead-letter queue fills, the ops warden finds the cause (Apple push, the Anthropic API, a data source, a deploy) and, once it is healthy again, replays the messages at most three times with growing gaps; a message that fails the same way again becomes an incident with its errors attached. What the system fixes on its own, and tells the admin in the digest: a failed step retried; a source failed over within its chain; a gap filled on the next run; a missed schedule caught up; a model or a calibration map rolled back to its last good version; a step that still fails skipped, its output labelled stale. Everything else is an Incident on the admin desk with what happened, what was tried and the choices. Every agent reports a heartbeat, and a silent one is an incident. The daily run is one trace and each agent a service on the map; a replay links to the run it repeats and an incident names its trace (§10.8, MA-235). The 07:45 dead man's switch, the nightly drift check (MA-108) and the Lambda rollback (MA-107) stay as they are.

20.8 Order

The settings store (E1) and the admin desk (E2) are useful long before the rest and can come forward once the Admin center and the second check exist (phase 8 and MA-102). The data steward (E3) follows phase 10's sources; grading and calibration (E4, E5) can start as soon as there are graded stances (phase 13); challengers (E6) need the Forecaster (phase 17); the ops warden (E7) and the agent budgets (E8) come with them. The household's learning (E9, E10, §20.9) needs the settings store (E1); E9 also needs closed months (phase 6, MA-220) and the Projection's runs (phase 7), and E10 the runs of phase 1. Whether they come forward with E1 and E2, ahead of the market side, is open on MA-276 and MA-277. One household, its one admin, and nothing shared: the bounds of §4.5 stand.

20.9 The household learns too: its forecasts graded each month, its needs learned from use (E9, E10; MA-276, MA-277)

Arun, 29 Oct 2026 (MA-275): "I need the entire app to be self learning based on my needs, the data tracked in database and S3 storage for all processing to calibrate and tune the system."

§20.4 learns from prices. The household side learns the same way from its own records: what it forecast is kept as made, graded when the truth arrives, and changed only on evidence, a step at a time, with the admin, or the member it concerns, deciding. The three rules of §20 hold, and so does rule 1: code works out every grade and every proposed value, and Claude explains them. Learning changes inputs, orders and defaults, never a record, a figure, the budget plan or the engine's arithmetic.

The household's forecasts, graded every month (E9, MA-276).

  1. Kept as made. The ProjectionRun current on the 1st of each month already holds its inputs hash, seed and engine version (§6.6), so that month's line of the Budget forecast can be read back exactly as it was.
  2. Graded at the month close. When a month closes (§4.9, MA-220), code compares that line with what happened: income by member, spend by category, EMIs, each plan item, the surplus and closing cash. Each gap is a MonthGrade row: the forecast, the actual, the gap in money and as a percentage, and the input it traces to (a category over its plan, a salary that changed, a category's prices rising faster than its country's, a spend no plan held). Once a year the Projection's net worth from twelve months earlier is graded the same way. A late statement regrades its month with a new row; none is overwritten.
  3. Proposed from the household's own history. Each month the tuner refits the inputs that are estimates, never the household's facts:
    • each category's run rate against its plan;
    • each category's own inflation, blended with its country's prior by the years of history behind it, as returns are (§6.6);
    • each member's salary growth, which §6.6 already blends with its prior, now checked against what was paid.

A value is proposed only with at least 6 closed months behind it, only if it would have forecast the months in its window (the last 12, or as many as have closed) better than the current value by more than a margin, and only a step at a time. The window, the minimum, the margin and the step are settings (§20.2).

  1. Decided by the admin. A proposal is a card in the admin's chat and on the Projection's assumptions panel (illustrative figures):
The tuner suggests
Groceries ran over its £450 plan in 7 of the last 9 months, by about £70.
Use £520 a month for Groceries in the Budget forecast from December?
The plan stays £450. Surplus: £1,180 a month becomes £1,110.
It would have forecast those 9 months £390 closer than the plan did.
Undo: back to the plan's £450; nothing else changes.
[Use £520] [Keep the plan] [Why?]

Assumptions are the admin's alone (§6.6), so it is applied only on the admin's Confirm, as a new version of ProjectionAssumptions with its audit row and Undo; like a plan item it changes a plan, not the records, so it needs no second check (§4.5). Members see proposals and cannot apply them. A rejected proposal is not offered again for the period its setting names; an applied one whose next three months grade worse than the value it replaced is offered back.

  1. Shown. The Budget forecast tab shows each closed month's forecast beside its actual, and the admin's monthly note (§20.4) says how close the last twelve months came. Designed first, with /design-first, before the story starts.

What each member needs, learned from use (E10, MA-277). MyPaisa already records what each member asks, opens, taps, confirms, corrects, cancels and mutes (the run records of §10.1 and the audit log of §4.6). From those it keeps daily counts per member, never message text, and learns:

  • Each member's learning is their own (§4.5): one member's habits never reorder another's screens; the admin sees the household's in the Admin center.
  • Only the member's own actions teach it. Text that a file, a page, a search or the news brought in teaches it nothing (§23.2), so a planted line cannot train it.
  • Nothing it sends changes without a Confirm, and nothing outside its band changes at all. You lists what was learned, with a reset. Designed first, with /design-first, before the story starts.

Where it lives. MonthGrade, LearnedProposal and MemberSignal in DynamoDB (§4.1); their snapshots in Parquet under household/<household>/learning/ in the lake (§20.6), encrypted and kept for good, read with DuckDB by the tuner and never by another household's query. Its own settings bound it (§20.2), and its jobs run at each month close and nightly (§23.6).
