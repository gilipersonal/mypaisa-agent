18. The agent team: configured, approved, self-healing, self-learning (phase 13, later)

Arun, 24 Oct 2026 (MA-127): "Keep everything configurable which I can control by Admin Web portal or telegram bots (only for admin) talking to Agents. The Agents should be able to ask me what is needed and ask for my approval for any kind of changes ... recalibrate and tuning you algorithms on daily basis ... This should be self healing and self learning system ... For any kind of issues, connect with the admin for approval ... This app is just for my household." The daily grading, recalibration and challengers he asked for there served the market side and were retired with it (MA-307, E4 to E6); the household's own learning stays (§18.8). And on 2 Nov 2026 (MA-307): "I need robust AI agents to do all this, as the budgeting and forecasting on my wealth will be dynamic."

Three rules hold for every agent. Code decides, Claude explains: models and rules produce every number and decision; Claude tags, writes, asks and answers. Nothing changes without a trace: every behaviour is a setting, and every change is a version and an audit row. Outside its band, it asks: an agent may act on its own only inside the automatic band its settings allow; anything else is a request to the admins.

Each agent is a Lambda with its own role, tools, model routing and budget share. They do not call each other directly: they publish events on the mypaisa-<env>-events EventBridge bus and leave work in DynamoDB, and the daily pipeline is a Step Functions state machine (§18.4). No agent can trade, read a secret, erase data or apply infrastructure: secrets are put in SSM by hand, and infrastructure changes only through Terraform and CI (.claude/rules/aws-changes.md); an agent can at most open a Jira issue proposing one.

18.2 Settings: one store for every behaviour (E1, MA-129)

Every behaviour the household might want to change is a Setting: the data source chains, the Projection's priors, schedules, alert budgets, ceilings and agent shares, and the learning loop's own bounds. Each has a schema with an allowed range, an automatic band (how far an agent may move it without asking; often zero), and an approval rule. Defaults live in config/defaults.yaml, validated in CI; the live values in DynamoDB, one ConfigVersion row per change, never edited, with a daily snapshot to S3. One settings service makes every change and enforces the schema, the range and the approval rule, so no agent can write a setting directly. Lambdas read settings through a cache of a minute at most. Secrets stay in SSM and infrastructure in Terraform: a setting may name a secret, never hold one.

18.3 The admin desk: the admins' chat and the Admin center, admins only (E2, MA-130)

The desk is the admins': several since MA-308, the owner always one of them; members and viewers never see it. Both doors show the same queue:

  • In the chat, in each admin's own conversation with MyPaisa, and by push. A request arrives as a card, for example:
Data steward asks
yfinance has not returned Balanced for 3 days; Twelve Data does not list it.
EODHD's free key returns its history back to 2019.
Switch on EODHD Historian at £19.99 a month?
AWS and others: £3 of £50 used this month.
Undo: switch it off; nothing else changes.
[Approve] [Reject] [Why?] [Later]
  • In the Admin center, its Approvals, Settings, Agents and Incidents screens (designed first).

Approving needs the second check (§4.5, MA-76): a fresh Sign in with Apple or passkey under Face ID, or for a member who signs in only with Google, Google again. Deciding in one place updates the other, and the first admin to decide settles it for all. Each request says what changes, from and to, why with its evidence, the cost and the budget left, the risk and the undo, and when it expires. Agents also ask plain questions ("Which fund is the Monzo Balanced fund's ISIN?") with buttons or a typed answer that resumes their work. An admin gives instructions in their own words ("put Twelve Data first for London", "lower dev to $30", "why is silver's price two days old?"); the concierge turns an instruction into the same request with a Confirm card, and a question into an answer from the agent concerned. Non-urgent requests wait for one daily digest; incidents come at once; a rejected request is not proposed again for the period its setting names. Every decision is audited.

18.4 Self-healing (E7, MA-135)

The daily run (the price ingest, the nightly views, the Projection run, the check-ins, the insight job) is one Step Functions state machine with retries and backoff on every step and idempotent writes, its jobs carried by the queues of §19. When a dead-letter queue fills, the ops warden finds the cause (Apple push, the Anthropic API, a data source, a deploy) and, once it is healthy again, replays the messages at most three times with growing gaps; a message that fails the same way again becomes an incident with its errors attached. What the system fixes on its own, and tells the admin in the digest: a failed step retried; a source failed over within its chain; a gap filled on the next run; a missed schedule caught up; a setting rolled back to its last good version; a step that still fails skipped, its output labelled stale. Everything else is an Incident on the admin desk with what happened, what was tried and the choices. Every agent reports a heartbeat, and a silent one is an incident. The daily run is one trace and each agent a service on the map; a replay links to the run it repeats and an incident names its trace (§9.8, MA-235). The dead man's switch (§9.6: the views and the Projection current by 07:00, the check-ins sent by 09:00 outside quiet hours), the nightly drift check (MA-108) and the Lambda rollback (MA-107) stay as they are.

The settings store (E1) is built in phase 9, with the household's learning below; the admin desk (E2) is useful long before the rest and can come forward once the Admin center and the second check exist (phase 8 and MA-102). The data steward (E3) follows phase 5's prices; the ops warden (E7) and the agent budgets (E8) come with the team in phase 13. E4 to E6 (grading predictions, recalibration, challengers) were retired by MA-307. The household's learning (E9, E10, §18.8) is built in phase 9 with the settings store (E1), straight after the whole wealth (Arun, 29 Oct 2026, MA-275: "Straight after phase 9"): E9 grades the months that phase 6 closes against phase 7's runs; E10 starts with the chat's chips, the merchant memory, check-in answers and corrections after a file, and learns the alerts once phase 10 brings them. One household, its admins, and nothing shared: the bounds of §4.5 stand.

18.8 The household learns too: its forecasts graded each month, its needs learned from use (E9, E10; MA-276, MA-277)

Arun, 29 Oct 2026 (MA-275): "I need the entire app to be self learning based on my needs, the data tracked in database and S3 storage for all processing to calibrate and tune the system."

The household learns from its own records: what it forecast is kept as made, graded when the truth arrives, and changed only on evidence, a step at a time, with an admin, or the member it concerns, deciding. The three rules of §18 hold, and so does rule 1: code works out every grade and every proposed value, and Claude explains them. Learning changes inputs, orders and defaults, never a record, a figure, the budget plan or the engine's arithmetic.

The household's forecasts, graded every month (E9, MA-276).

  1. Kept as made. The ProjectionRun current on the 1st of each month already holds its inputs hash, seed and engine version (§6), so that month's line of the Budget forecast can be read back exactly as it was.
  2. Graded at the month close. When a month closes (§4.9, MA-220), code compares that line with what happened: income by member, spend by category, EMIs, each plan item, the surplus and closing cash. Each gap is a MonthGrade row: the forecast, the actual, the gap in money and as a percentage, and the input it traces to (a category over its plan, a salary that changed, a category's prices rising faster than its country's, a spend no plan held). Once a year the Projection's net worth from twelve months earlier is graded the same way. A late statement regrades its month with a new row; none is overwritten.
  3. Proposed from the household's own history. Each month the tuner refits the inputs that are estimates, never the household's facts:
    • each category's run rate against its plan;
    • each category's own inflation, blended with its country's prior by the years of history behind it, as returns are (§6);
    • each member's salary growth, which §6 already blends with its prior, now checked against what was paid.

A value is proposed only with at least 6 closed months behind it, only if it would have forecast the months in its window (the last 12, or as many as have closed) better than the current value by more than a margin, and only a step at a time. The window, the minimum, the margin and the step are settings (§18.2).

  1. Decided by an admin. A proposal is a card in the admins' chat and on the Projection's assumptions panel (illustrative figures):
The tuner suggests
Groceries ran over its £450 plan in 7 of the last 9 months, by about £70.
Use £520 a month for Groceries in the Budget forecast from December?
The plan stays £450. Surplus: £1,180 a month becomes £1,110.
It would have forecast those 9 months £390 closer than the plan did.
Undo: back to the plan's £450; nothing else changes.
[Use £520] [Keep the plan] [Why?]

Assumptions are an admin's alone (§6), so it is applied only on an admin's Confirm, as a new version of ProjectionAssumptions with its audit row and Undo; like a plan item it changes a plan, not the records, so it needs no second check (§4.5). Other members see proposals and cannot apply them. A rejected proposal is not offered again for the period its setting names; an applied one whose next three months grade worse than the value it replaced is offered back.

  1. Shown. The Budget forecast tab shows each closed month's forecast beside its actual, and the tuner's monthly note to the admins says how close the last twelve months came and what changed and why. Designed first, with /design-first, before the story starts.

The fan, graded too (MA-319). E9 grades the median path, the Budget forecast's line; the fan around it (§6.4) is a claim about how far the truth may stray, and is graded as one, so trust grows from the household's own record ("the band has held 9 of the last 10 quarters").

  1. Coverage. At each month close, for the ProjectionRuns current 1, 3 and 12 months earlier, code checks whether the household's actual net worth, and each goal's pools (§6.1), landed inside that run's p10 to p90 for this month. Each check is a MonthGrade row (line: the fan's coverage, with its horizon). The coverage rate over a rolling window is the share inside; a well-made band holds about 80% of the time.
  2. PIT. For each check, the percentile at which the actual fell inside that run's distribution (its probability integral transform) is stored. Once there are 12 or more, the histogram shows whether the band is too wide (actuals bunched in the middle), too narrow (piled at the edges) or biased (piled on one side). A household makes 12 monthly points a year, so the early reading is labelled with how few points stand behind it.
  3. Glide paths. Each goal's pools are graded against the glide path saved with the goal: the gap, and whether it is inside its band. The insight job reads the same rows (§10.1).
  4. Priors re-fitted only on evidence. The tuner proposes a change to a return, volatility or inflation prior (§6.4) only when the coverage has left its band (below 60% or above 95%, settings) over at least 12 points and the PIT shows the direction; only a step at a time, inside the prior's own band (§18.2); and only on an admin's Confirm, as a new version of the prior with its audit row, as item 4 above. A household's few points never move a prior on their own: the step is small and the prior's source is shown beside the proposal.
  5. Shown. The tuner's monthly note gains one line, how often the band has held and over how many points; the Projection's assumptions panel shows the same line, and its Technical part the PIT histogram (drawn with /design-first before MA-319 starts).

What each member needs, learned from use (E10, MA-277). MyPaisa already records what each member asks, opens, taps, confirms, corrects, cancels and mutes (the run records of §9.1 and the audit log of §4.6). From those it keeps daily counts per member, never message text, and learns:

  • Each member's learning is their own (§4.5): one member's habits never reorder another's screens; admins see the household's in the Admin center.
  • Only the member's own actions teach it. Text that a file, a page, a search brought in teaches it nothing (§21.2), so a planted line cannot train it.
  • Nothing it sends changes without a Confirm, and nothing outside its band changes at all. You lists what was learned, with a reset. Designed first, with /design-first, before the story starts.

Where it lives. MonthGrade, LearnedProposal and MemberSignal in DynamoDB (§4.1); their snapshots in Parquet under household/<household>/learning/ in the lake (§18.5), encrypted and kept for good, read with DuckDB by the tuner and never by another household's query. Its own settings bound it (§18.2), and its jobs run at each month close and nightly (§21.6).
