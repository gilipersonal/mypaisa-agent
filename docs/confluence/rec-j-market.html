🎯 What can and cannot be forecast

The words of this section

1

The honest starting point

MA-117ARCHITECTURE §19.1

Risk and mood can be forecast; tomorrow's up or down cannot. Calm and rough spells cluster, so how rough the next few weeks will be is far more forecastable than which way prices go. A tool claiming 80 to 90% accuracy on direction has learned its past by heart.

Can

  • How rough the next few weeks will be, and the chance of a deep fall: risk is far more forecastable than direction.
  • The market's mood: calm and rising, choppy, stressed or falling.
  • What usually followed days like today, and how a holding moved after past events of a kind.
  • A modest, honest edge on direction over weeks, not days.

Cannot

  • Tomorrow's up or down: short-term moves are mostly noise.
  • Rare shocks: wars and pandemics have too few past cases for confident numbers.
  • Candles or opening prices for OEICs: they price once a day and deal at the next day's price.
  • Anything faster than the household can act on.

Rough days follow rough days

Each bar is one day's move. The band is twice the last three weeks' volatility. The size of the moves comes in spells, so next month's roughness can be estimated; the sign of tomorrow's bar is close to a coin toss.

+4%−4%calmrough spell: big moves both wayscalm again

An ETF or a share: candles

It trades through the day, so each day has an open, high, low and close. The Forecast page draws candles for ETFs and shares, an NSE or BSE share or ETF (in_listed, phase 19) among them.

A UK fund (OEIC): one price a day

Its NAV is struck once a day and published the next morning, and a deal goes through at the next day's price. No candles, no opening price: one dot a day. An Indian mutual fund (in_mutual_fund, phase 5) is the same: one NAV a day, from AMFI by the UK evening.

So the horizon is four weeks (20 trading days), the first aim is avoiding deep falls, and direction is the last and least trusted piece.

Up candles are green with the day's gain; falls are quiet ink. The figures are illustrative.

The data steward: failover and gap repair

Code keeps the prices flowing; Haiku only writes its requests to the admin.

  • Fails over within the chain when a source answers nothing or stale data, and marks the valuation provisional.
  • Watches freshness, gaps, free quotas, and disagreement: when two sources serve one instrument, a close more than 0.2% apart, a missing day or a late correction is listed.
  • Repairs a gap on the next run and labels anything it could not fill as stale; a stale series is left out of scoring.
  • Raises a stale feed as an alarm in the chat; the 07:45 alarm fires only when every source has failed for two days.
  • Asks the admin for a new or reordered source, any paid feed, or a source's terms.

The holder's cascade

The desk's cascade rotated capital; this one accumulates with care. Adverse macro and elevated risk tighten the framing and lower confidence; they never change the pillar numbers. Sentiment may tilt the action one notch only when the price pillars do not contradict it. News alone never triggers a red alert.

The confidence score

confidence = clamp(0.5 + agreement + persistence + quality + history, 0, 0.95)

  • Agreement: +0.05 for each pillar pointing the same way, −0.05 for each against.
  • Persistence: bars the state has held, 0 for 1 bar up to +0.10 for 10 or more.
  • Quality: −0.10 for partial components, stale or provisional data, or a proxy gap over 1%.
  • History: the hit-rate of past calls in the same state, once 20 or more exist, pulling the score toward it (phase 13).

🎓 Grade every day, calibrate every day, change rules on evidence

The words of this section

7

The learning loop, daily

Phase 13: the graderE4 MA-132E5 MA-133E6 MA-134

The grader and tuner runs the loop for both engines. Every prediction is kept exactly as it was made, with the versions of its model and settings and the date its inputs were known, and graded against what prices actually did.

  1. Keep it as made Every stance and forecast, with its model and settings versions and the date its inputs were known. S3 predictions/
  2. Grade after each close For each horizon that has passed: the realised return, hit or miss, Brier score, log loss, inside its range or not. A late correction adds a regrade; nothing is overwritten. S3 outcomes/ScoreDaily
  3. Calibrate within bounds Refit on a rolling window; used only if better on the held-back part, only where a bucket has enough cases, at most 2 points per bucket per day. CalibrationMap
  4. Probation 10 trading days. A map that does worse is rolled back on its own.
  5. Report A line in the admin's daily digest, the scoreboard in the Saturday review, a monthly note on what changed and why.

What a grade measures

ScoreWhat it says
Brier scoreThe average of (chance said − what happened)², where what happened is 1 or 0. Lower is better; always saying 50% scores 0.25.
Log lossPunishes being confidently wrong far harder than being unsure.
Range coverageHow often the outcome fell inside the 10th to 90th percentile range: it should be about 80%.

Why the rules themselves are not retuned daily

A day's outcomes are mostly noise, and a system that chases yesterday learns noise. Calibration moves a little every day; structure moves only on weeks of evidence and the admin's approval. That is how accuracy, and above all honest confidence, grow over the years.

The strategy layer · where the goal is won

F7 MA-125after F2 and F3

Forecasts become suggestions about the monthly contributions. It shows as the suggested split on the first-of-the-month contribution prompt, beside the rules engine's. The app cannot trade: the household reads the suggestion and makes any move itself.

The rules it keeps

  • Contributions always continue. Money keeps going in every month; the question is only where.
  • New money tilts away from stress: from holdings in a stressed or falling mood or with a high chance of a deep fall.
  • A trend filter: a holding below its long-run trend gets less of the new money until the trend turns. The exact rule is F7's to design and the lab's to test.
  • Rebalancing within bands, done with the next contributions rather than by selling.
  • A drawdown budget the admin sets: the deepest fall the household is willing to sit through.
  • Never "sell everything", stays ISA-safe, and only suggests. For Indian accounts (phase 19) "ISA-safe" becomes "wrapper-aware": a suggestion names only actions that suit its account.

Self-healing on the market pipeline

MA-138MA-139MA-140E7 MA-135MA-137

A bad minute at a data source, the Anthropic API or Apple's push service costs a delay, never a day's work. From phase 18 the daily run is one Step Functions state machine with retries and backoff on every step and idempotent writes, its jobs carried by the SQS work queue.

  1. Ingest Prices, the economy, events, through each chain.
  2. Engine The rules: signals and stances.
  3. Forecaster The six models, one Forecast row per holding.
  4. Grade Every prediction whose horizon has passed.
  5. Calibrate Within bounds, on probation.
  6. Brief Always attempted; late inputs go out labelled stale.

Each step retries with backoff and writes idempotently. A step that still fails is skipped and its output labelled stale; the rest of the run goes on.

Scroll sideways to see the whole drawing.

Fixed on its own, told in the digest

  • A failed step retried.
  • A source failed over within its chain.
  • A gap filled on the next run.
  • A missed schedule caught up.
  • A model or a calibration map rolled back to its last good version.
  • A step that still fails skipped, its output labelled stale.

Raised as an incident

Everything else is an Incident on the admin desk, at once, with what happened, what was tried and the choices. Every agent reports a heartbeat, and a silent one is an incident.
