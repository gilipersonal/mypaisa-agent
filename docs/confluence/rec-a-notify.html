11. Notifications, news, calls

As v1, now per member and by push (§9.1, MA-170): Apple push to the iOS app, and Web Push to an installed web app (MA-200), each only to a device with no fresh presence on the stream (§21.5) and with no amounts on the lock screen unless the member chooses. The Web Push key (VAPID) is a write-only SSM parameter (§14). The 07:15 brief is written once for the household and once for each member who wants one, each in their own preferences; the 21:15 wrap goes to whoever's accounts moved; tiered alerts (red with sound, max two a week per member; amber silent; digest), cooldowns, quiet hours, Haiku repeat filter, all per member. News from RSS, scored nightly by Haiku into a sentiment index; event calendar (MPC, FOMC, ECB, CPI, earnings of top holdings). Stances are paper-traded and graded; they appear only in the Saturday review until the record beats the baseline for 8 weeks, then in weekday cards.

Check-ins (§4.9, phase 4) arrive the same way, decided by code. Several due at once share one card; autopay commitments are assumed paid after their grace period and listed in a weekly digest (MA-211) instead of one notification each. Together they keep the household to about ten notifications a week, two with sound at most (§2).

12. The road to MyPaisa, the wealth tracker

The dashboard of §9.3 grows into the household's whole wealth tracker, first, in PLAN's phases 2 to 9: one layer per phase, each shipped at both doors and ending in a demo Arun can click, and in an update to the agent-journey explainer. The current wealth-tracker repository, a Next.js static export over two invented seed families that resolves everything from fixtures and has no backend, holds no real data: nothing persists, and its sign-in is simulated. What it has is worth keeping: well-tested TypeScript maths (xirr.ts, amortisation.ts with its prepay modes, forecast.ts, budget.ts, currency.ts; 646 tests) and a complete set of screens. So its maths is ported with its cases as golden fixtures and its screen list kept as the check on what the new dashboard covers (MA-105, phase 3); its screens are rebuilt here, on this backend, with a new design. The family's real data arrives through the chat, statements and the Excel history importer, never from the old tracker. The old repository is retired in phase 9 (MA-97). What stays fixed:
Create in the chat, correct in placeAnything new (a record, an event, a kind, a plan item) is created only in the chat, with a Confirm tap and an audit row (§9.1): every property valuation, EMI, rent, maturity, prepayment and plan item. An existing figure its kind marks editable is corrected in place on any screen, through the same write service and with its audit row (§4.10). The api's data routes stay GET only, apart from the correction route, PATCH /v1/records/{id}. The Admin center holds only the owner's admin settings and operations (§9.3): the security actions, the ceiling and caps, replays, agents' requests and settings, incidents, each behind a second check. Years of history come in through the Excel importer, sent in the chat (MA-111).
One backendThe same DynamoDB table, entities (§4.1), identity (§4.5), audit log (§4.6) and observability (§10). The first savings record of phase 2 and the household's property, deposits, policies, pensions, investments and loans are records of one structure (§4.8).
One identityThe members and roles of §4.5, with figures masked for a viewer as the tracker masks them for a standard user. Apple, Google or a passkey to sign in, through each environment's Cognito pool.
Two currenciesGBP and INR side by side, from the ECB's daily rates, chosen per member (phase 3); USD and EUR for the holdings that trade in them.
Numbers from codeXIRR, schedules, net worth, the budget and the Projection from §6.6; Claude writes the words around them and answers "why is my net worth down this month?" from the same tools.

12.1 Any question, answered from a read-only SQL copy of the ledger (MA-104)

Arun, 24 Oct 2026: "I will be making such queries in future about my transactions and investments." Questions nobody planned for ("average monthly dividend per member over two years", "how much went into ISAs versus pensions since 2024") are answered without a database running all month:

  • A nightly job exports the household's ledger (the records, their events, the views such as net worth, and the valuations) to S3 as Parquet, under that household's prefix, encrypted, with a set retention.
  • A query_ledger_sql tool runs DuckDB inside the Lambda over that household's files only. Claude writes the SQL from the question; the query is read-only (SELECT only), stops at 10,000 rows or 10 seconds, and cannot reach another household's prefix. DuckDB runs with external access off and its configuration locked, so a query cannot read a URL or a local file, over views of the asker's own scope, so a member sees only what they may see (§4.5, §23.2).
  • The figures in the answer come from the result, never from the model, and the SQL is shown under the answer's Technical section so every answer can be checked. The card is designed first.
  • The structured query_ledger tool (MA-62) stays the first choice; this one is for the questions it cannot express. Optionally (MA-104), the same files can be read with Athena or with DuckDB on the owner's laptop; either needs a read-only role of its own for that prefix, since no human role reads prod's records today (§18.1; the AWS MCP reads only metadata, §18.4), and the role is decided when the story starts.
