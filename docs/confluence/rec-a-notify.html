10. Notifications

Per member and by push (§8.1, MA-170): Apple push to the iOS app, and Web Push to an installed web app (MA-200), each only to a device with no fresh presence on the stream (§19.5) and with no amounts on the lock screen unless the member chooses. The Web Push key (VAPID) is a write-only SSM parameter (§13). Alerts are about the household's own money and the system: a budget category past its plan, a check-in overdue, a job that failed, a spend step reached (MA-55, MA-61). They are tiered (red with sound, at most two a week per member; amber silent; a digest), with cooldowns, quiet hours and a Haiku repeat filter, all per member.

Check-ins (§4.9, phase 4) arrive the same way, decided by code. Several due at once share one card; autopay commitments are assumed paid after their grace period and listed in a weekly digest (MA-211) instead of one notification each. Together they keep the household to about ten notifications a week, two with sound at most (§2). The insight job's digest and nudges (§10.1) count inside that, with no sound.

10.1 The insight job: quiet nudges and a Monday digest (phase 10, MA-316)

MA-307 took away the only voice MyPaisa had that spoke unasked with a reason: the morning brief and the Saturday review. What stayed were code's check-ins and threshold alerts. The insight job gives back a proactive voice inside the rules: it notices what matters in the household's own numbers and says it as facts, rarely.

A nightly worker job, insights, runs after the Projection run in the daily run (§18.4), from the views, the commitments, the goals and the latest run. Its detectors are code, each with its threshold a setting (§18.2):

DetectorFires when
Budget driftA category's run rate this month is past its plan by more than its band
Idle cashCash above a number of months of the household's spend (six to start)
A goal off its glide pathA goal's pools below its glide path by more than its band (§6.1)
A maturity soonA deposit or policy maturing within 30 days, with what its sum is
A rate change soonA fixed rate ending, or a floating rate's reset, within 90 days, with the EMI before and after on the stated terms
A prepayment's impactA loan where a stated prepayment would change the interest or the end date by more than a band: a computed comparison, never "you should"
An unusual spendA statement line far outside its merchant's and category's history
A salary not creditedAn income commitment past its date and grace with no credit
A currency moveGBP/INR past a band since the last digest, with what it did to the Indian records (§6.2)
An allowance's year endAn allowance with room left within 30 days of its year's end (§6.5)

Scored and kept quiet. Each finding is an Insight (§4.1) with its facts and their traces, a fingerprint so the same finding is never sent twice, and a score: impact (money at stake against the household's monthly spend) × urgency (days to act) × novelty (not said before, from the per-run self-audit's list, §7) × the admin's past engagement with that kind (E10, §18.8). Quiet by default:

  • One digest card on Monday morning in each admin's chat, silent, sent by 09:00 outside the admin's quiet hours: what changed last week and why (the attribution's parts, §6.2), what is coming in the next fortnight (get_calendar), and the week's best-scored findings. It replaces nothing and joins the weekly digest of assumed autopay check-ins (§4.9) as one card.
  • At most one timely nudge a week per admin, only for a finding whose urgency cannot wait for Monday (a salary not credited, a maturity in under a week), amber and silent unless the admin's own settings say otherwise (§14.2).
  • Muted kinds are learned. Each card has Mute for its kind; a mute is a MemberSignal count (§18.8), the kind stops for that admin at once, and E10 offers it back as a preference card after a period the setting names. Nothing else changes without that admin's Confirm.

Admins only, as budget alerts and check-ins are (§14.2): a finding is about the household's records, which only an admin writes. A member who is not an admin asks "what changed this week?" in the chat and gets the same facts from explain_change and get_calendar.

Words. Haiku writes each finding's one line and Sonnet the digest, from code's facts; every figure passes the figure check and every cause the claim check (§6.2). No nudge says "should" or tells the household to buy, add, hold, pause, trim or sell a holding (§1, rule 2); each kind has its safety and capability cases for "fact, not advice" (§21.3). From the 85% step the digest goes and timely nudges wait; from the 95% step the digest goes as its plain template with code's figures (§14.1). The job counts inside the ten notifications a week of §2, with no sound.

11. The road to MyPaisa, the wealth tracker

The dashboard of §8.3 grows into the household's whole wealth tracker, first, in PLAN's phases 2 to 9: one layer per phase, each shipped at both doors and ending in a demo Arun can click, and in an update to the agent-journey explainer. The current wealth-tracker repository, a Next.js static export over two invented seed families that resolves everything from fixtures and has no backend, holds no real data: nothing persists, and its sign-in is simulated. What it has is worth keeping: well-tested TypeScript maths (xirr.ts, amortisation.ts with its prepay modes, forecast.ts, budget.ts, currency.ts; 646 tests) and a complete set of screens. So its maths is ported with its cases as golden fixtures and its screen list kept as the check on what the new dashboard covers (MA-105, phase 3); its screens are rebuilt here, on this backend, with a new design. The family's real data arrives through the chat, statements and the Excel history importer, never from the old tracker. The old repository is retired in phase 9 (MA-97). What stays fixed:
Admins write: add and delete in the chat, correct in the chat or in placeAnything new (a record, an event, a kind, a plan item) is added only in the chat, with a Confirm tap and an audit row (§8.1): every property valuation, EMI, rent, maturity, prepayment and plan item; a delete too, with the second check, marking the record deleted (MA-309). An existing figure its kind marks editable is corrected in the chat or in place on any screen, through the same write service and with its audit row (§4.10). Only admins write; every other member views (MA-308). The api's data routes stay GET only, apart from the correction route, PATCH /v1/records/{id}. The Admin center holds only the admin settings and operations (§8.3): the security actions, the ceiling and caps, replays, agents' requests and settings, incidents, each behind a second check. Years of history come in through the Excel importer, sent in the chat (MA-111).
One backendThe same DynamoDB table, entities (§4.1), identity (§4.5), audit log (§4.6) and observability (§9). The first savings record of phase 2 and the household's property, deposits, policies, pensions, investments and loans are records of one structure (§4.8).
One identityThe members and roles of §4.5, with figures masked for a viewer as the tracker masks them for a standard user. Apple, Google or a passkey to sign in, through each environment's Cognito pool.
Four currenciesGBP, INR, USD and EUR, from the ECB's daily rates, chosen per member for every figure (phase 3, Arun, 29 Oct 2026, MA-275); each record keeps its own currency.
Numbers from codeXIRR, schedules, net worth, the budget and the Projection from §6; Claude writes the words around them and answers "why is my net worth down this month?" from the same tools (explain_change, §6.2).

Budget: statements plus monthly check-ins (phase 6). Income is each member's take-home salary and its history (past years' figures, so growth can be measured), from the chat or payslips, kept as income commitments that accept dated future changes so phase 7's plan items can change them (MA-96, MA-261). The plan is a monthly amount per category; categories are data the household changes by chat, each carrying its inflation key, and EMIs and card payments come from the commitments. The actuals come from statements, categorised and reviewed with one Confirm, and from the check-in on the 1st, which asks for what no statement covered (§4.9). Every amount is take-home, with no tax maths. The screen shows plan against actual per category, income, EMIs, surplus and savings rate, per member and for the household, in GBP, INR, USD or EUR.

11.1 Any question, answered from a read-only SQL copy of the ledger (MA-104)

Arun, 24 Oct 2026: "I will be making such queries in future about my transactions and investments." Questions nobody planned for ("average monthly dividend per member over two years", "how much went into ISAs versus pensions since 2024") are answered without a database running all month:

  • A nightly job exports the household's ledger (the records, their events, the views such as net worth, and the valuations) to S3 as Parquet, under that household's prefix, encrypted, with a set retention.
  • A query_ledger_sql tool runs DuckDB inside the Lambda over that household's files only. Claude writes the SQL from the question; the query is read-only (SELECT only), stops at 10,000 rows or 10 seconds, and cannot reach another household's prefix. DuckDB runs with external access off and its configuration locked, so a query cannot read a URL or a local file, over views of the asker's own scope, so a member sees only what they may see (§4.5, §21.2).
  • The figures in the answer come from the result, never from the model, and the SQL is shown under the answer's Technical section so every answer can be checked. The card is designed first.
  • The structured query_ledger tool (MA-62) stays the first choice; this one is for the questions it cannot express. Optionally (MA-104), the same files can be read with Athena or with DuckDB on the owner's laptop; either needs a read-only role of its own for that prefix, since no human role reads prod's records today (§17.1; the AWS MCP reads only metadata, §17.4), and the role is decided when the story starts.

12.1 The backend's layers (MA-24)

Arun, 3 Nov 2026: "best practices for FAST API, controllers, services, repository and whatever is the latest and greatest. Proper Gates using ruff, black etc". The code lives in four top-level folders, one per part (Arun, the same day: "I need to code to be structured backend/ web/ ios/ infra/"): backend/, web/, ios/ and infra/. The backend is a uv project of its own in backend/ (pyproject.toml, uv.lock, its tests), and its Python package backend/src/mypaisa_agent/ is cut into four layers whose imports point one way, api -> services -> repositories -> domain, with core (settings and the error classes) beside them:

LayerHoldsMay import
api/ (controllers)FastAPI routers under /v1/, request and response schemas apart from the domain, dependency wiring from the app the request reached, the app factory, and service errors mapped to RFC 9457 problemseverything below; the only layer that knows HTTP
services/The use cases: take repositories by constructor, return domain objects, raise core.errorsrepositories, domain, core
repositories/The ports as Protocols; the adapters implement them (the DynamoDB single-table registry of §4.8 and MA-270, S3, SSM), so a service never imports boto3 and a test passes an in-memory fakedomain, core
domain/Entities and value objects as frozen Pydantic v2 modelsnothing with I/O: no settings, no boto3, no anthropic

lint-imports (import-linter) refuses an import against the arrow, an HTTP import below api/, and an I/O import in domain/. The gates are one script, scripts/backend_gates.sh, run by the jira-flow backend gate, by CI's Backend job and by hand: uv lock --check, ruff check with every rule on and a few named exceptions, ruff format --check (Black-compatible, so Black itself is not run), pyright strict on the package and the tests, lint-imports, pytest with branch coverage of at least 90%, and pip-audit; pre-commit runs the same tools on every commit. The app is built by create_app(settings, probes); /v1/health says the process is up and /v1/health/ready asks every readiness probe at once, each within a timeout, and answers 503 naming the one that failed (for the smoke runner, MA-268). The Lambda handlers, the correlation middleware and OpenTelemetry wrap this app in MA-266; CLAUDE.md holds the working rules.
