4. State: the holdings registry

The user said holdings will change and the system must save all state. The registry is designed so that adding a new kind of thing adds data, not code. Since v4 the household's own records are one dynamic structure: kinds as data, records as documents, history as events, figures as views (§4.8). The entities below hold the prices, identity, the agents and the operations beside it, and say where a v3 entity now lives inside that structure.

Prices are per instrument and shared by everyone who holds it; a holding belongs to one member's account.

Every item carries schema_version; a migration is a Lambda that rewrites items of an older version, run once from a script, never by hand. The household's records need none: a kind's new version is data, and its views are rebuilt from the events (§4.8).

4.2 Adding a holding, from the chat

The file goes straight from the phone to the documents bucket through a presigned upload, and every hop after that goes through a queue (§19): the message waits in the member's lane, the document read is a job on the work queue run by the worker, and every reply goes out through the outbox to the member's stream at every open door (§19.5), or by push when none is open. The sequence in Lucid: open. It adds a fund from its factsheet as a record (draft_record, ask_user and the Confirm card, §4.8), then corrects a figure in place at the other door; both go through the one write service, each change with its audit row in one transaction (§4.10).

Any field Claude is unsure of is shown in amber and asked about. A holding is never saved without the Confirm tap. The same path handles an ETF (ISIN and ticker from the factsheet), a share (ticker and exchange), or cash, and since v4 any kind of record, from a sentence, a screenshot or a statement (§4.8).

4.6 The audit log

Every change to the records leaves a row. Not a log line that may or may not be there, a row in the same DynamoDB transaction as the change, so a change without its audit row cannot happen. Each row says who (the member, or the system job), when, through what (a typed sentence, a button, a screenshot, a factsheet, a proposal from the agent, a schedule), which record, what it was before and what it is after, and which run made it. Rows are chained by hash, so a deleted or edited row breaks the chain and the weekly check says so.

What counts as a change: records and their events, holdings, positions, transactions, accounts, corrections in place (via: inline_edit), check-in answers, kinds, commitments, plan items and saved assumptions, a record deleted or restored (MA-309), members, invites and the admin role (MA-308), preferences, and every valuation a person supplied. The nightly computed valuations are a time series, not audit rows; they are already dated and sourced, and logging each one would bury the rows that matter.

Who sees what follows the roles in §4.5: a member sees the rows for their own accounts and for the household-level records; an admin sees every row. The rows are readable in two places: the Audit screen at every door, with filters by member, record and date, and from the chat with a question such as "what changed this week?", which the agent answers through get_audit. Rows are never deleted; at a few hundred a year they cost nothing to keep.

Every file is kept, and opens from the log (Arun, 24 Oct 2026: "all the files and images I send should be stored on S3 and I should be able to view that in audit logs"). Every file or image a member sends (a factsheet, a screenshot, a CAS statement, a broker export, a spreadsheet) is copied to s3://mypaisa-<env>-documents/<household>/<member>/<yyyy>/<mm>/ before anything reads it, whatever happens next, with a Document row and an audit row of its own; every change made from it carries its document_id (MA-148). Nothing is deleted after reading: the bucket has no expiry rule, and prod's is versioned and protected from destruction. On the Audit screen a row made from a file shows its kind, name and size, a thumbnail for an image, and Open; a Files view lists every file with the changes it fed (MA-149). Open is a presigned link valid five minutes, given only after the authorizer checks the role: a member opens their own files, an admin any, a viewer none; a deleted record's files stay kept and open from its rows; each opening is itself an audit row, and the file opens in Quick Look. In the chat a change card's Source button opens the original.

4.7 Money in any currency, checked against the holding (MA-156)

Arun, 24 Oct 2026: "On the telegram App, I should be able to add transactions using Any currency for any fund and chatbot to figure what the base currency of the holdings, If the holdings are in GBP and I try to invest INR to GBP holding, chat should be able to correct me."

Every holding has one currency: the one it is bought and valued in, as the platform shows it (Position.ccy). It is read from the factsheet or the screenshot when the holding is added and shown on that Confirm card, so it is confirmed once and never guessed again. A fund's own base currency (Instrument.base_ccy, often USD) can differ from the share class a member holds (GBP); the position's currency is the one that counts here.

  • A converted figure is never Claude's. The ECB publishes each currency against the euro, so pounds to rupees is the engine's cross of the two euro rates of that day. It is marked an estimate until a statement, a screenshot or the next valuation confirms it. The button appears once the daily rates are loaded (MA-44, phase 3); before then it is left out and the question is the same.

Privacy mode shows no amount and no rate on the card, only words, currencies and dates (the rate with one amount would give the other away); Show amounts reveals one card.

4.8 The dynamic structure (kinds, records, events, views)

Arun, 26 Oct 2026 (MA-186): "Keep the entire structure dynamic. This is not a traditional transaction SQL structure." Four ideas carry it. They are built in phase 2 (MA-27, MA-28, MA-197) and every wealth phase after it adds only kinds.

  1. Kinds are data. A versioned kind spec (Kind, §4.1) holds:
    • the fields: typed; required, optional or derived; and which are editable in place (§4.10);
    • the valuation method;
    • the events it accepts;
    • its commitment and check-in templates (§4.9);
    • its dashboard grouping: class, liquidity, country;
    • how it is presented.

The valuation methods and event handlers are a fixed, tested code library: last figure, accrual, amortising, revolving, market-priced, and the PPF, EPF, NPS, SGB and LIC schedules (§6). A spec only combines them. A new kind, or a new field on a kind, is data: the agent can propose one from the templates ("Sukanya Samriddhi: accrual at its rate to 21 years"), an admin approves it behind the second check (propose_kind, §4.5, §7), and it is versioned. No screen, form or tool is added.

  1. Records are documents. Record {kind, kind_version, owners[{member, share_pct}], country, ccy, liquidity, status, parent_id?, instrument_id?, attrs{}}. An ISA account is a record, and its funds are child records. Instrument stays the shared price reference (§4.1).
  2. History is events. Event {record, type, effective_date, payload{}, source, actor, document_id?}:
    • append-only;
    • hash-chained with the audit log (§4.6);
    • never edited: a correction or a reversal is a new event (§4.10);
    • a record's current state is the fold of its events through its kind's handlers.
  3. Figures are views. Each record's value series, NetWorth, the budget month, the commitments due and the Projection runs are materialised from the events. They can be rebuilt after a spec changes, so the structure evolves without a migration.

Account, Position, Liability and Transaction (§4.1) fold into Record and Event for the household's own records. There is no relational schema: questions nobody planned for read the nightly Parquet exports of the events and views through DuckDB (§11.1, MA-104). In code (src/mypaisa_agent/registry/, MA-27, MA-28, MA-265, MA-270), the single table is accessed through a thin typed repository on Pydantic v2, boto3 (TypeSerializer and TypeDeserializer with Decimal conversion at the wire boundary) and mypy-boto3-dynamodb (boto3-stubs[dynamodb]) under strict pyright, with a TransactWriteUnit builder that refuses to commit a mutation without its hash-chained AuditEvent, a client guard that refuses Scan, and HMAC-signed opaque cursor tokens for pagination. Amazon DocumentDB and ORM layers such as PynamoDB are not used: DocumentDB costs £75 to £110 a month across dev and prod and needs a VPC, whereas DynamoDB's native Map and List attributes, TransactWriteItems (up to 100 items) and single-table Query run inside the free tier over stateless HTTPS (docs/RESEARCH.md §6.2).

The model covers these cases:

CaseHow
Dated loan termsA floating-rate reset, or a fixed rate that ends and turns variable, is an event dated when it takes effect
PrepaymentShortens the term or lowers the EMI, defaulting by country
A loan or a pot that started years agoA balance checkpoint, so it can start part-way through without its history
CardsRevolving debt, kept from their statements (§4.9)
Joint recordsTwo owners with their shares; a check-in, sent to the admins, names the payer, and both owners see it pending
An FD's on_maturityRenew, renew with interest, or pay out; a renewal starts a successor record linked to the old one
SIPs and RDsCommitments, with an annual step-up
Closureclosed, matured or sold; separate from a delete
A record added by mistakeDeleted through the chat: marked deleted, hidden from every view and total, its events, audit rows and files kept for good, restorable through the chat (MA-309, §4.10)

The flow, whatever the kind:

  1. A sentence, a screenshot or a statement comes in.
  2. The agent drafts the record with draft_record, or something that happened to one with draft_event, passing each field as a name and the member's own text. Code parses and types every value against the spec, derives what it can (an EMI, a maturity, an end date) and returns what is missing or uncertain.
  3. The agent asks only what is missing, one thing at a time, with chips. It never asks what it can look up.
  4. A Confirm card shows the figures code computed. The draft is hashed in PendingAction, and Confirm commits exactly that draft through the write service (§4.10).
  5. The event and its audit row are written in one transaction.

An admin registers for any member, and the card names whose it is; a member who is not an admin views only. PendingAction holds several open flows, keyed by flow id, so a check-in arriving mid-way does not wipe a half-registered FD.

The kinds arrive layer by layer: savings, cash, a property's value and a pension's value in phase 2; savers, fixed and recurring deposits, PPF, EPF, NPS and policies in phase 3; loans, mortgages, EMIs and cards in phase 4; shares, ETFs, OEICs, mutual funds, bonds and gold, in the UK and India, in phase 5.
