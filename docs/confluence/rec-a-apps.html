9. The iOS app

One app, MyPaisa, for iPhone and iPad on iOS and iPadOS 26 and later, with a MyPaisa Dev build that points at dev (MA-161, MA-165), built as a React Native shell with a native SwiftUI core, as bong-panjika is built (§9.5). It is the chat and the dashboard in one: the member talks to MyPaisa, and the household's figures are a tap away. Beside it, the web app (§9.4) is the same chat, dashboard and Admin center in a browser, at desktop and phone width, as a PWA. There is no bot, no Mac app and no Android app (Arun, 25 Oct 2026: "Only iOS app is needed and nothing else for now"; "we have webapp for the dashboard and admin portal pwa"); on 26 Oct 2026 (MA-186) both doors became full doors, built in parallel in every phase, and the app's measure is Arun's "Performance is key … silky smooth like Monzo". Its screens are drawn in Claude Design, in the MyPaisa iOS v1 project on the iOS kit of MyPaisa DS v1 (docs/design/README.md); the decision record is docs/design/reviews/MA-161/decision.md, and the whole-wealth screens are drawn by MA-193.

9.1 The shell, the chat and the size classes

The shell (MA-163) is 1a, Chat first, Arun's pick on 26 Oct 2026 from three options drawn on Shell options.dc.html: the app opens to the chat, the household's total rides in a glass strip at the top, and pulling it down opens Home. The other two stay on the board as the record: 1b Ask from anywhere (the app opens to Home, and the composer rides on every tab as the tab bar's bottom accessory) and 1c One timeline (the chat is the app, under a rail of pinned live cards).

  • Tabs on iPhone: Chat · Home · Activity · You, with Search as iOS 26's separate search tab (MA-180); the owner opens the Admin center from You. The tab bar is the system's own, Liquid Glass, hosted by the shell through Fabric (§9.5), and shrinks to its selected tab as content scrolls.
  • The composer: a glass field with a + for the camera, a document scan (VisionKit), Photos and Files (PDFs, images, workbooks), and shortcut chips above it that follow the conversation (Portfolio, Today, Add a holding, Log money, Ask about Balanced). The commands still work when typed: /add, /update, /why, /whatif, /calls, /scorecard, /technical, /privacy, /pause, /resume, /help; for the owner /invite and /members open their Admin center screens.
  • The conversation: the member's messages in navy bubbles; MyPaisa's words unbubbled beside its mark, streamed word by word while it writes (§21.5); cards as Warm ledger cards with their own buttons, drawn natively from the agent's versioned JSON (MA-174), the same JSON the web app draws with Svelte components, every figure in them from code. Every card has a plain top and a Technical fold; any term in a technical section explains itself on a tap.
  • Household and Me on the Portfolio card and on Home: one tap switches between the household and your own accounts; the owner also gets a tab per member.
  • Charts in Swift Charts, one house style (MA-56): the GBP axis, the stance horizon shaded, drawn from the series the card or the screen carries, with an audio graph for VoiceOver.
  • Two modes: simple (default) and technical (every card expanded), per card with its fold or everywhere from You or /technical.
  • Guided flows: logging a transaction, adding a holding, registering any record (§4.8), answering a check-in that needs more than a tap (§4.9), updating a valuation and changing a setting each run as a short series of questions, one card per question, edited in place so the conversation stays tidy; answers are chips, Pick a date opens the system's date picker, and anything already said is skipped. Long lists (many holdings) become a short list plus More and Type the name. A flow that gets no answer for a day is dropped quietly.
  • Push (MA-170): the brief, the wrap, alerts, check-ins, and replies that finish while the app is closed arrive as notifications; a red alert is time-sensitive with sound, amber is passive; no amount shows on the lock screen unless the member turns that on, and never with privacy mode on.
  • Offline (MA-173): the last Home, holdings and conversation stay on the device (file protection complete) and show their time; a message typed offline waits and goes once the network is back; Confirm needs a connection.

Devices and size classes. The app lays itself out by width, never by device, so it fits every iPhone, every iPad in every multitasking size, and a foldable or two-panel device (Arun's "Duo"), folded or open:

The rule is the window's width, never the device, with one exception: an iPhone is always compact, its tab bar in either orientation, as option 1a draws it (iPhone · Landscape). Widen past about 900 pt and the sidebar opens beside the screen; widen past about 1133 pt and the sheet becomes the trailing column, narrow below it and the column becomes the sheet, with matched geometry and no reload (the boards of iPad and foldable.dc.html). Arun, 27 Oct 2026 (MA-240): from 900 to 1133 pt the screen beside both the sidebar and the chat fell to about 360 to 480 pt, too narrow for the screens as drawn, so there the chat is a sheet away. A fold, a rotation or a resize keeps the conversation, the scroll position and any guided flow. With a hardware keyboard, Return sends, ⌘K focuses the composer, ⌘1 to ⌘5 open the sections and ⌘F searches. Every screen works from xSmall to AX5 Dynamic Type, with VoiceOver, Increase Contrast, Reduce Transparency and Reduce Motion (MA-181).

Legible at 25% brightness (MA-181). Arun, 25 Oct 2026: "the text and component should be clearly visible even in 25% mobile brightness". Every colour pair in light and dark, text on glass included, meets two limits, one at full brightness and one with the screen at 25%: primary text 7:1 and 4.5:1, secondary and coloured text 4.5:1 and 3:1, meaningful marks (chart lines, bars on their track, a switch's ring) 3:1 and 1.8:1, and what must only be seen (a privacy mask, an empty track) 1.5:1 and 1.25:1. scripts/contrast_check.py measures every pair in MyPaisa DS v1's tokens.css and the glass fills of ios.css, fails closed, and is a gate on every PR; the app's snapshot tests render every screen in both themes. Glass carries labels only, never body copy, at a fill dense enough to hold them over the brightest content.

Motion (MA-182). One motion system for every component and chart, in the app and the web app, drawn on the iOS kit's 12 · Motion board and shared by every board through motion.css and motion.js. Content moves, the glass bars stay put: cards and rows arrive with SwiftUI's .smooth spring and a 45 ms stagger (at most eight); controls answer a touch with .snappy; only a success (Saved, Face ID passed) uses .bouncy. The composer, the tab bar's lens and the attachment menu morph through a GlassEffectContainer. Every figure is a numericText content transition: it counts up once when it first appears, and afterwards only its changed digits roll. Charts draw in under a mask, their dots and labels landing after, and scrub with a glass lollipop and a selection haptic at each point; a range change morphs the series. A holding row zooms into its screen (navigationTransition(.zoom)); messages push in from the bottom; the streamed words fade in as they arrive; a red alert's mark pulses once. Haptics: success on Saved, a warning on a refusal, selection on chips, segments and scrubbing, a light impact on Send. Nothing loops forever, and under Reduce Motion every movement becomes a short cross-fade or lands at once. The web app plays the same choreography with Motion 14 (springs tuned to the same curves), Svelte transitions, CSS transitions and View Transitions (MA-229).

The look: Warm ledger on Liquid Glass. MyPaisa DS v1, Warm ledger (MA-101), stays the system: Monzo's warmth on Apple's HIG. Its tokens become the app's colour assets (light, dark, increased contrast), its type scale is Dynamic Type, SF Pro Rounded carries the hero figures and SF Symbols the icons. Liquid Glass is the layer of controls above the content, as Apple's guidelines say: the tab bar, the sidebar, toolbar buttons, the composer and its chips, menus and partial sheets. Content (the navy hero, the cards, lists, charts and messages) stays solid; glass never sits on glass; only a group's primary control is tinted coral; red stays for alerts that ask for action. The app icon is the 1c Watcher mark (MA-103) in Apple's layered format, with a DEV band on MyPaisa Dev.

9.3 The dashboard, inside the app

Since v4 the dashboard is the household's whole wealth, built a layer per phase (phases 2 to 9): Overview, Assets and one asset, Debts and one loan, Budget and Projection, beside Home, a holding, Overlap, Scorecard, Audit and Files, Activity, Search and, for the owner, the Admin center. They are screens of the same app (MA-78, MA-79, MA-95), built from the boards in MyPaisa iOS v1: one spec, drawn for iPhone and for iPad and the foldable, in light and dark (docs/design/README.md). Arun, 25 Oct 2026, asked for the dashboard "on the ios tablet duo" too: every one of these screens is laid out for the iPad and the open foldable too, with the chat beside it from about 1133 pt, a sheet away from about 600 to 1133 pt, and a tab in a compact window (every iPhone, a folded foldable, Slide Over) (§9.1). The web app shows the same screens in a browser (§9.4).

The screens, where anything new is made in the chat and a figure its kind marks editable is corrected in place (§4.10):

  • Overview (from phase 2, complete in phase 9): net worth in GBP or INR, switchable per member, with the ECB rate and its date and the change since last month; liquid now, at maturity and locked-in upside; where it sits; what we owe and how it runs off; this month's cash flow; invested against owed and where they cross; by member and by country (UK and India); maturing next; where growth comes from; the largest assets; and what's due (EMIs, maturities, open check-ins, §4.9).
  • Assets and one asset (savings, property and pensions from phase 2, deposits and policies from phase 3, investments in phase 5): filters by kind, member, country and maturity window; one asset's value, its XIRR with the working, its event timeline and its files.
  • Debts and one loan (phase 4): the outstanding total, the EMIs and the interest left, by member; one loan's balance against its plan, the yearly split, the end date, prepayment what-ifs from the engine, its events and its files.
  • Budget (phase 6): plan against actual per category, income, EMIs, surplus and savings rate, per member and for the household, in GBP or INR (§12).
  • Projection (phase 7): the fan in GBP or INR, in today's money or not, opening at 20 years with 5, 10, 20 and 30 to choose; milestones on the line; the assumptions panel (the admin's floor among them), each figure with its source and editable by the admin; the Plans tab (each plan on a timeline, corrected or paused in place by the admin, Add in chat) and the Budget forecast tab (each year to the horizon, its leanest month against the floor first, expandable to months, every figure opening its flows); scenario chips; Ask (§6.6).
  • Correction in place on any of these: tap a figure its kind marks editable, type the new value, see a before → after line, Save; it is saved as a correction event with its audit row (via: inline_edit). Nothing can be created in place (§4.10).
  • Home, a holding, Overlap, Scorecard: as the boards, with the Household / Me / member switch from §4.5. A holding's screen has Why?, What if?, Ask about it, and Update value, which opens the chat with the guided flow already started (MA-80); a figure on it can also be corrected in place. On an iPad or a foldable the chat sits beside them from about 1133 pt, is a sheet away from about 600 to 1133 pt, and is a tab in a compact window (every iPhone, a folded foldable, Slide Over) (§9.1).
  • Audit: the audit log of §4.6, newest first, filtered by member, record and date; each row shows before and after and links to the run that made it, and a row made from a file opens that file in Quick Look; Files lists every file sent (MA-149).
  • Activity: what the agent and the jobs did (§10), from phase 1. Each run with its trigger, the model calls and their tokens and cost, the tools it called and how long they took, what it proposed and sent, and any error; a board of the scheduled jobs with their last and next run and the freshness of each holding's price; this month's Claude spend against the ceiling and the step it is on; alarms from the last seven days.
  • Search (MA-180): records, holdings, audit rows, files and the member's own past answers, within the member's rights.

Every figure comes from the api at api.mypaisapp.com (api-dev for MyPaisa Dev), whose authorizer checks the access token, the member's status and session epoch on every call, so removing or pausing a member locks them out at once. A change confirmed at any door reaches every open door on the stream as records.changed, and the figures move (§21.5). The app keeps the last figures for offline use, encrypted at rest by iOS's file protection, and shows their time.

Nothing new is created outside the chat, in the app or on the web. The api's data routes are GET only, with one exception: PATCH /v1/records/{id} takes a correction to a field its kind marks editable (and the admin's plan items and saved Projection assumptions, §6.6), through the write service (§4.10). No screen creates a record (the CAS password of §22.2 is typed only to open a file on the device, and changes nothing); everything new still goes through a card and a Confirm tap in the chat, where it is audited. The other exceptions are the owner's admin settings and operations in the Admin center: the security actions (sign out a device or everywhere, pause, restore, unlink; MA-102); the Claude ceiling with its member share, renewal day and hard limit, the caps, their alert thresholds and the alert routing, quiet hours and cooldowns (§15.1, §15.2, MA-116, MA-137); replaying dead-lettered work (§21.3, MA-145); approving or rejecting an agent's request and rolling a setting back (§20.2, §20.3, MA-130); choosing how an incident ends (§20.5, MA-135); and on the Evals page, labelling runs for the judges, turning a failed run into a test case and moving the eval caps (§23.5, §23.7, MA-255). Each is behind the second check (§4.5), writes an audit row, and none changes the household's records. Privacy mode hides pounds and rupees here too. Each environment has its own api host (api-dev, api), with the stream on it (§21.5), and its own dashboard site (dashboard-dev, dashboard), §18.

9.4 The web app: the dashboard and the Admin center in a browser

Arun kept the web app on 25 Oct 2026 (MA-161) for the dashboard and the Admin center; on 26 Oct 2026 (MA-186) it became a full door with the chat, built in every phase beside the iOS app. One SvelteKit 3 app (Svelte 5 runes, adapter-static, a single-page app) in web/, at dashboard.mypaisapp.com (dashboard-dev for dev), installable as a Progressive Web App on a laptop, an iPad or a phone (MA-73, MA-194), at desktop and phone width.

  • The chat (MA-199): the same conversation as the iOS app, one per member, streamed over SSE (§21.5); every card drawn by Svelte components from the same versioned JSON (MA-174), with its chips and Confirm, Edit and Cancel; files and photos through a presigned upload, kept for good (MA-148); text typed offline kept and sent once the browser is back online. At desktop width the chat sits beside the dashboard; at phone width it is a tab. A flow started on the laptop finishes on the phone.
  • The pages: Overview, Assets, Debts, Budget, Projection, Home, a holding, Overlap, Scorecard, Audit and Files, Activity, and for the owner the Admin center: the screens of §9.3 with the same correction in place (§4.10), from the browser boards in the MyPaisa app v1 Claude Design project, at desktop and at 390 (the phone-web door; the earlier boards picked on MA-70 before MA-161, with the sign-in page redrawn for Apple, Google and a passkey; MA-183, MA-184, MA-185; the whole-wealth screens drawn by MA-193), in Warm ledger, with the motion of §9.1. Two columns at laptop width; the adaptive shell of MyPaisa DS v1 below it.
  • The stack (MA-229, the one Smart Clearance's frontend runs, at its latest): SvelteKit 3.1, Svelte 5.57 and Vite 8.3 in TypeScript 6.0, a pnpm 12 workspace; Tailwind CSS 4.3 with its theme mapped onto the Warm ledger tokens; shadcn-svelte 1.7 components on bits-ui 2.20, owned in web/src/lib/components/ui/; Lucide icons; TanStack Query 6.3 (the SSE stream writes into its cache), Table 9.3 for the lists, Virtual 3.13 for the chat and long lists, and Form 1.33 for the in-place correction and the Admin center's settings (still no creation form); shadcn-svelte's charts on LayerChart 2.9 for the dashboard, and lightweight-charts 5.2 only for the market side's price charts; Motion 14 and Svelte transitions. Tests: Vitest 5, Playwright 1.64 with axe, and a pixel parity check of each screen against its board.
  • Shared code: the card types, the OpenAPI 3.1 endpoint types generated from FastAPI via openapi-typescript (web/src/lib/api-types.d.ts, MA-266), the formatting (GBP and INR with lakh and crore, privacy masking) and the view models live in web/src/lib/, written once and imported by path by the iOS app's shell (§9.5).
  • Sign-in (MA-185): Sign in with Apple, Google or a passkey, as §4.5's browser row; the api sets the HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict session cookie for its own host (thirty days on a laptop), with five-minute access tokens behind it; the dashboard and the api are the same site, so no token is ever visible to page scripts, and the cookie reaches the stream on the api host; CORS admits only that environment's dashboard (and localhost in dev). The Admin center's actions need the second check (§4.5): a passkey or a fresh sign-in.
  • The PWA: a manifest with the app icon, a service worker that caches the shell and nothing else (no figure is kept on a shared laptop), standalone display, and an install prompt only after a second visit. Web Push reaches an installed web app when it is closed (§11, MA-200). Privacy mode hides pounds here too.
  • One site, two jobs: the dashboard also serves the iOS app's apple-app-site-association file (its applinks for the links, and its webcredentials, so a passkey made in the app signs in to the web app; §4.5), so an invite link (https://dashboard.mypaisapp.com/join/<token>) or a link to a holding opens the iOS app on a phone that has it, and the web page anywhere else (MA-73). The file is web/static/.well-known/apple-app-site-association, uploaded with Content-Type: application/json. There is no S3 error-page fallback: a CloudFront Function sends extension-less page paths to the app's shell and leaves /.well-known/ and every real file alone, so the association file is always served as itself (MA-229).
  • Hosting: S3 and CloudFront per environment, with the existing wildcard certificate and strict security headers; the Content-Security-Policy carries the hashes SvelteKit generates for its inline scripts, so it needs no unsafe-inline; the static shell is public and holds no data; every figure comes from the api, whose authorizer checks the session, the member's status and session epoch on every call.
  • Budgets: INP at most 200 ms and LCP at most 2.5 s on a mid-range phone; 60 fps scrolling; Playwright screenshots at 390 and 1440 in both themes on every PR (§18.4).

9.5 The iOS app's build: a React Native shell with a native SwiftUI core

Arun, 26 Oct 2026 (MA-186), asked for a native SwiftUI core inside a React Native shell, as bong-panjika is built: "Performance is key … silky smooth like Monzo". It follows bong-panjika's frontend-native-apple, with its swiftui-first and native-apple skills adapted here (MA-165, MA-166).

  • The shell: bare React Native 0.88 with React 19.3, the New Architecture, Hermes, TypeScript 6.0, no Expo (MA-229). It owns the navigation (the system tab bar, UITabBar, through Fabric), the store, the api and SSE client (§21.5), sign-in orchestration, push registration and the shared models.
  • The core: every piece a member looks at is SwiftUI, hosted through a Fabric component and fed one formatted JSON model:
    • the chat timeline and its cards;
    • the composer;
    • the hero and the figure rows;
    • the correction in place (§4.10);
    • Swift Charts;
    • the sheets.

The shell formats; the SwiftUI piece only draws, so a scroll never waits on JavaScript.

  • The look: Liquid Glass and the iOS kit of MyPaisa DS v1, as already picked (§9.1).
  • Shared TypeScript: the card types, the formatting (lakh and crore, privacy masking) and the view models are written once in web/src/lib/ (§9.4) and imported by path, through Metro's watchFolders and a #lib alias.
  • Smoothness has a budget, in a PERFORMANCE.md with its benches, measured on the phone:
    • 0 dropped frames at 120 Hz scrolling the chat and the dashboard, and as cards arrive;
    • a chart scrub at 120 Hz;
    • a launch budget.
  • Tests (MA-166): Jest for the shell; XCUITest, snapshot and accessibility checks for the SwiftUI pieces.
  • The shell's libraries (MA-229, bong-panjika's, at their latest): TanStack Query 5.105 for React on the same query core as the web app, using the query definitions in web/src/lib/; Reanimated 4.7 with Worklets 0.13 and Gesture Handler 3.4 for the shell's own motion and gestures; MMKV 4.3 on Nitro Modules 0.37 for the offline store. Tailwind and shadcn are the web's look; here every pixel stays SwiftUI, from the same tokens.
  • Dependencies: Swift Package Manager from day one, through React Native 0.88's react-native spm (opt-in and hardened in 0.88); never CocoaPods, whose trunk goes read-only on 2 Dec 2026. A native library that cannot build with Swift Package Manager is replaced (MA-229).
  • Bundle ids are unchanged (com.mypaisapp.agent, com.mypaisapp.agent.dev).

This replaces v3's app built in SwiftUI alone with XcodeGen; what a member sees, the size classes, the motion and the 25% brightness rule of §9.1 are unchanged.
