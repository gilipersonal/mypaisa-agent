🔑 Joining and signing in

2 of the Agent Journey's flows, each as a numbered path across five lanes (the member, the app, AWS, Claude and external data), the screens the member sees, the safeguards on the path and the Jira stories that build it.

01 · Joining by invite

The owner types /invite in the chat. MyPaisa answers with a one-time link, which the owner shares from iOS's share sheet. Asha taps it on her own iPhone, MyPaisa opens on the invite, and signing in with Apple or Google makes her a member of the same household, with her own chat, her own accounts and her own preferences.

Trigger the owner types /invite (or /invite viewer) Phase 1

🛤️ The path

#LanesWhat happens
1Member to AppYou type /invite in the chat
2App to AWSThe app posts it to API Gateway, POST /v1/chat/messages, with a client id and your access token
3AWSThe authorizer checks the token, your status and your session epoch. api λ puts the message on the SQS FIFO updates queue in your lane (the client id dedupes) and answers 202 at once
4AWSagent λ knows you from the token, checks you are the owner, and writes an Invite to DynamoDB: single use, 24-hour expiry, this household, this role. Its audit row goes in the same transaction
5AWS to AppThe reply goes on the SQS outbox; sender λ delivers it over the stream: a card with the link https://dashboard.mypaisapp.com/join/<token> and Share
6Member to AppYou tap Share; iOS's share sheet sends the link to Asha in Messages, WhatsApp or Mail
7Member to AppAsha taps it on her iPhone. iOS opens MyPaisa through the universal link (the apple-app-site-association file the web app's site serves on dashboard.mypaisapp.com); a phone without the app gets the site's static join page, which names nothing
8App to External (Apple)She taps Sign in with Apple and passes Face ID; Apple returns its identity token with the api's one-time nonce inside it
9App to AWSThe app sends the token and the invite to /auth/apple
10AWSCognito's custom-auth Lambdas check Apple's token against Apple's published keys, then the invite: unused, under 24 hours old, this household. It is marked used; Asha's Member row and her Cognito user in mypaisa-prod-users are created, linked to her Apple account's stable id, each with an audit row
11AWS to AppHer tokens go to the Keychain; the welcome and the first of three setup questions arrive over her stream
12Member to AppAsha answers with the cards' buttons: base currency, what she holds, how often to be told things; then iOS asks to allow notifications

👀 What the members see

  • Your chat. The one-time link; Share opens iOS's share sheet.
  • Asha's iPhone. The link opens MyPaisa on the invite; signing in creates her member.
  • Asha's chat. Three setup questions, one card at a time; then iOS asks to allow notifications.

🛡️ Safeguards on this path

A one-time link

Single use, a 24-hour expiry, bound to one household and one role. Shared outside MyPaisa, it is worth nothing once used or expired.

The join page names nothing

dashboard.mypaisapp.com serves the file that lets iOS open the app and a static join page; nothing before sign-in names the household or a member, and every figure is behind the api.

Only signed-in members

The chat API refuses any message without a valid token; the client id drops a duplicate.

Strangers are refused

An Apple or Google account linked to nobody, and carrying no valid invite, is refused at sign-in.

The audit row

The invite, the new member and her Cognito user each leave a row in the same transaction as the change.

No passwords

Password sign-in and self sign-up are off in Cognito. A user exists only because someone joined through an invite.

Nothing lost on the way

Every message travels the FIFO queue in its member's lane, in order, retried and dead-lettered if it keeps failing.

🧩 Built by

The first person to sign in owns the household and invites and removes members with one-time links
Signing in takes one tap with Apple or Google, the session stays in the Keychain, and an invite link opens the app to join
Each environment hosts the web app at dashboard(-dev).mypaisapp.com on S3 and CloudFront, with the iOS app's association file and the invite page
The Apple Developer Program, and the MyPaisa and MyPaisa Dev app ids with Sign in with Apple, push and associated domains
The chat answers onboarding, add, update, privacy, pause, resume and help, typed or from its shortcut chips
Each member's records have their owners, and a joint record has two owners with their shares
The chat API accepts only a signed-in member's messages and taps, and hands each off once
Every record change writes a hash-chained audit row in the same transaction

02 · Signing in with Apple, Google or a passkey

There are no passwords. In the app, Apple or Google vouches for the member, natively, and the environment's Cognito user pool turns that into a short-lived session kept in the Keychain. In the web app it is Apple, Google or a passkey, and the session is a cookie the api sets. An import, a rollback, leaving the household and every admin action, from the Admin center or the admin's chat, need a second check, bound to that one action: a fresh Sign in with Apple or a passkey under Face ID in the app (Google again for a Google-only member), a passkey or a fresh sign-in in the browser.

Trigger opening MyPaisa on a device that is not signed in, opening dashboard.mypaisapp.com in a browser, or an action that needs the second check Phase 1 (sign-in and the second check, in the app and the browser)

🛤️ The paths

Route 1: Sign in with AppleApple's identity token, exchanged at /auth/apple for Cognito tokens

#LanesWhat happens
1Member to AppAsha opens MyPaisa. The welcome screen shows Apple's and Google's own buttons, Apple's first, and nothing else
2App to AWSThe app asks the api for a one-time nonce
3App to External (Apple)She taps Sign in with Apple; iOS shows Apple's sheet and she passes Face ID. No scope beyond the id is asked for
4External (Apple) to AppApple's identity token, with the SHA-256 of the nonce inside it
5App to AWSThe app sends it to /auth/apple; the api starts Cognito's custom auth for the member that Apple account is linked to
6AWSThe verify trigger checks the token against Apple's published keys: issuer, audience (the app's bundle id), expiry, an issue time under five minutes, the nonce, and no replay. An account linked to nobody is refused, unless it carries a valid invite (flow 1)
7AWS to AppCognito's tokens: a five-minute access token and a refresh token that rotates on use, kept in the Keychain, this device only, after first unlock
8App to AWSThe app registers the device and its push token (POST /v1/devices) and opens the stream, /v1/stream on api.mypaisapp.com
9AWSOn every /v1 call and on the stream the authorizer checks the token, Asha's status and her session epoch; the data routes accept a correction only to a figure its kind marks editable. Each sign-in is an audit row
10AWS to AppA new device: Asha's chat asks Was this you?, and the owner gets a message with Open Admin center

Route 2: Sign in with GoogleGoogle's iOS sign-in, with PKCE, exchanged at /auth/google

#LanesWhat happens
1Member to AppAsha taps Sign in with Google
2App to External (Google)Google's iOS SDK opens Google's own sign-in in a secure system sheet, with PKCE and the api's nonce
3External (Google) to AppGoogle's ID token
4App to AWSThe app sends it to /auth/google
5AWSThe same verify trigger: Google's keys, the audience (this environment's Google iOS client id), expiry, issue time, nonce, no replay. Google's stable account id (sub) maps to the member it was linked to, never the address. A Google account linked to nobody is refused
6AWS to AppEvery door ends the same way: Cognito's tokens in the Keychain, the device registered, an audit row

Route 3: the web app in a browserApple, Google or a passkey, through the same /auth exchange as the app; the session is an HttpOnly cookie set by the api

#LanesWhat happens
1Member to Web appAsha opens dashboard.mypaisapp.com on a laptop: CloudFront serves the static shell and its sign-in page, with no data in it
2Web app to AWSThe page asks the api for a one-time nonce, as the app does
3Web app to External (Apple or Google)Sign in with Apple through Apple's JS (this environment's Services ID as the audience), or Google through Google Identity Services (this environment's Google web client id), with the nonce
4External (Apple or Google) to Web appApple's identity token or Google's ID token, back to the page
5Web app to AWSThe page posts it to /auth/apple or /auth/google, the same exchange as the app, with no client secret. Or a passkey: a one-time challenge from /auth/passkey/options, signed under Face ID or Touch ID, sent to /auth/passkey
6AWSThe api starts Cognito's custom auth for the linked member, and the verify trigger checks the token against Apple's or Google's published keys (audience, expiry, issue time, nonce, no replay), or the passkey's signature against its stored public key, the challenge, the origin and the relying party, the web app's own domain. The account's stable id maps to the member, never the address; an account linked to nobody is refused
7AWS to Web appThe api sets the session: an HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict cookie for its own host, 30 days on a laptop; the five-minute access tokens behind it are never seen by page scripts
8AWSEvery /v1 call from the page carries the cookie; the authorizer checks the session, status and epoch, and CORS admits only this environment's site. Each web sign-in is an audit row, and a new browser gets Was this you? in her app's chat

Route 4: the second checkA fresh assertion, bound to one action, verified by the server

#LanesWhat happens
1Member (owner) to AppYou tap an action that needs it: Pause Asha in the Admin center (or an import's Confirm, or a rollback)
2App to AWSThe app asks the server for a challenge bound to that action
3AWS to AppThe challenge: one use, five minutes, this action only
4App to External (Apple or Google)A fresh Sign in with Apple with the challenge as its nonce; iOS shows the Face ID sheet. Or a passkey under Face ID; a Google-only member signs in with Google again. In the web app: a passkey, or a fresh sign-in
5External (Apple or Google) to AppThe fresh assertion
6App to AWSThe action and the assertion together
7AWSapi λ accepts the action only with an assertion under five minutes old whose nonce is that challenge; then it acts, with its audit row. A local Face ID check alone never authorises anything on the server

📌 The session, the same behind every door

Access tokens

Five minutes, refreshed silently with the refresh token, which rotates each time it is used.

The Keychain

The tokens live in the Keychain, this device only, available after first unlock: never in the app's files and never in a backup.

Every call

Token, member status and session epoch checked on every HTTP call and on the stream, so removing or pausing a member locks them out at once.

The cookie, in the web app

HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, set by api.mypaisapp.com for its own host; 30 days on a laptop. Page scripts never see a token.

Two environments

MyPaisa Dev and MyPaisa are separate apps, with their own bundle ids, sign-in settings and user pools, and each has its own web app. Tokens from dev are refused by prod; CORS admits only that environment's site.

A second sign-in

From You, Sign-in methods, a member adds Apple or Google, or a passkey, to their own identity while signed in, so the person who proves it is the member. Every link and unlink is an audit row.

Each device and browser

A Device row: name, model, last seen, push token; a browser session is listed beside them. A member signs their own out from You; the owner signs out one or all of a member's from the Admin center.

Face ID, two ways

The optional app lock (Face ID or the passcode on opening and after five minutes away) is a comfort on the device. The second check is the server's: a fresh sign-in bound to the action.

👀 What the member sees

  • Welcome. Apple's and Google's own buttons and nothing else; nothing before sign-in names a member.
  • Apple's sheet. Face ID, then Apple's token goes to /auth/apple; MyPaisa never sees a password.
  • The web app, in a browser. Three doors; nothing before sign-in names a member.
  • The second check. A fresh sign-in bound to one action: Face ID on the device, the proof on the server.
  • A new device. The member is asked; the owner is told.

🛡️ Safeguards on this path

Tokens, checked properly

Apple's or Google's token against their published keys: issuer, audience, expiry, an issue time under five minutes, the api's nonce, a replay refused.

By account id, not by address

Apple's and Google's stable ids are the key. A renamed Gmail still works; an address handed to someone else does not.

The Keychain, and the cookie

In the app, tokens on this device only, after first unlock, with a refresh token that rotates on use. In the browser, an HttpOnly cookie; page scripts never hold a token.

The second check

A fresh Sign in with Apple or a passkey in the app (Google again for a Google-only member), a passkey or a fresh sign-in in the browser: bound to one action, under five minutes old, verified by the server. Never a local-only Face ID.

Was this you?

A new device is asked about at once, and the owner can end it from the Admin center.

Refused, in one line

An account linked to nobody with no invite; a removed member; a paused member; an expired or used invite. Each is drawn and tested.

The audit row

Every sign-in, every sign-out, and every link and unlink of a sign-in method is recorded.
