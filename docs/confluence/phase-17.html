A second engine that learns from ten years of history and gives its own view of each holding beside the rules engine, graded on the same track record, as ARCHITECTURE.md §19. Epic MA-117. The goal is Arun's: fewer deep falls and steady growth, so market mood and risk come first and direction last.

  • F1, data (MA-119). Ten years, from 2015, of prices (each holding, the funds' top constituents, the indexes they track), the economy (FRED, Bank of England, ECB), market stress (VIX, credit spreads, dollar, oil, gold), world events (GDELT counts and tone, the geopolitical risk index) and the calendars, in Parquet with DuckDB; every row stamped with the date it was known; a daily refresh with a stale-feed alarm. Sources and prices as ARCHITECTURE.md §19.2: free first, EODHD Historian (£19.99 a month) for London, UK funds by ISIN and the back-fill, its free key tried on each fund first. Each holding's exposure map: a fund's holdings, countries, sectors and currencies; a share's company, sector, markets, suppliers and rivals; a commodity's drivers.
  • F2, the backtest lab (MA-120). Any strategy replayed day by day with fees, OEIC next-day pricing and monthly contributions; worst fall, worst year, time underwater, growth and calibration against buy-and-hold and a trend rule. First report: today's rules engine over ten years.
  • F3, mood and risk (MA-121). The market's mood each day and, per holding, four-week volatility and the chance of a fall over 5%, calibrated; in shadow.
  • F4, similar days and event impacts (MA-122). The 30 most similar days since 2015 and what followed; the measured effect of rate and inflation surprises, elections, conflict, tariffs, pandemics and earnings misses on each holding; Haiku tags each day's news with the companies, countries, sectors, commodities and central banks it names, its event type and its direction, and code scores each story against each holding's exposure map, so the brief can say which stories touch which holdings (§19.3).
  • F5, direction (MA-123). The chance of being up in four weeks, the likely range and its drivers, combined with F3 and F4 and calibrated; monthly retraining; model versions with their test scores; it switches itself off when its live record slips.
  • F6, the side-by-side view (MA-124). Designed first: the chat's card beside the rules engine's call, with disagreement flagged, and a Forecast screen per holding in the app; shown for a holding only after 8 live weeks beating the base rate and the rules engine, in the Saturday review until then.
  • F7, risk-steered money (MA-125). Contributions continue, tilted away from stressed holdings, with a trend filter, rebalancing bands and a drawdown budget the owner sets; shown as the suggested split on the first-of-the-month prompt.
  • F8, patterns and the technical set (MA-280, ARCHITECTURE.md §19.4). Algorithm and pattern driven, as you asked on 29 Oct 2026: code finds candlestick patterns (engulfing, hammer, doji, morning and evening star, inside bars) and chart patterns (breakouts, support and resistance, double tops and bottoms, head and shoulders, trend channels) on the daily candles of ETFs, shares and commodity ETCs, and line patterns on an OEIC's daily NAV, which has no candles; a wider indicator set (ADX, stochastics, on-balance volume, Ichimoku, Donchian and Keltner channels); each pattern measured per holding as an event study over ten years, fed to F3 and F5, and shown on a card only where it has an edge.

Order. F1 and F2 can start any time after phase 10, and F2 tests the rules engine early. F3 and F4 next (F4 needs phase 14's news), with F8 beside them (it needs F1's candles and F2's lab), then F5; F6 needs a holding's market view (phase 12, MA-183) and the track record (phase 13); F7 needs F2 and F3.

Tests: no feature used before the date it was known; a strategy that peeks at tomorrow caught by the lab; walk-forward folds with a gap and the last two years held out; calibration curves; a known-answer backtest to the penny; the event study against a hand-worked example; each pattern detector against hand-marked charts, and no pattern reading a bar after its date (MA-280); the switch-off; the card and the screen from fixtures, with snapshot tests.

Exit: over ten years of walk-forward tests and 8 live weeks, the risk forecasts beat the base rate; the steered contributions cut the worst fall and the time underwater against buy-and-hold, within the growth trade-off you set; both engines' views and records sit side by side in the chat and on the dashboard.

Cost: the data is free apart from EODHD Historian, £19.99 a month; compute a few dollars a month; Haiku filing the news under a dollar, inside the Claude ceiling.
