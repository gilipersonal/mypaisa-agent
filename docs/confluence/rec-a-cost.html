15.1 The Claude budget and its ceiling

Arun, 24 Oct 2026 (MA-112): "I have on prod a budget of $100 per month ... It should be configurable for me to move the ceiling up/down from Admin portal." Raised the same day (MA-127, MA-137): "Claude Spend Budget will be £200, I have Max x20 plan which give me that. Threshold to be set and kept configurable which I can control from Web portal Admin." AWS and others "will be separate £50".

The allowance is the plan's credit. The Max 20x plan's API credit is $200 a month, about £154 at $1.30, rather than £200; the defaults stay inside it so nothing reaches a card: a prod ceiling of $160 and a dev ceiling of $40, each a setting the admin moves. Ceilings are in dollars, as the credit and the Console are; the Admin center shows pounds beside them at the day's ECB rate. With no payment method and auto-reload off, the credit itself is the last stop. Going past $200 (to a full £200, about $260, say) means adding a card on the Console organisation, so when prod's and dev's ceilings together would pass the credit, the Admin center warns that the extra needs a card and asks for an explicit yes (MA-116). A third workspace, evals, holds the live evals with its own $30 limit, on top of the two ceilings (Arun, 28 Oct 2026): the three limits sum to $230, the credit stays the last stop with no card on file, and the warning still compares only prod's and dev's ceilings (§23.7).

What $160 buys. Two members are expected to spend about $24 a month on the standard setup and $39 on the full one (the Claude lines above, in dollars, about £18 to £30), plus a few pounds once the Forecaster and the agents of §20 run. The ceiling is a guard against a bad month or a runaway loop, not a ration.

The ceiling, set by the admin. BudgetSettings holds the monthly ceiling ($160 in prod, $40 in dev), the steps of the ladder (70%, 85%, 95%, 100%), each member's share (60%), the daily guard (three times the daily average), the calls per run (eight), each agent's share (§20.7), the eval caps (prod's overnight judges at 3%, pausing from the 85% step; the evals workspace's live evals at $10 a run and $30 a month; §23.7), and the day the plan's credit renews, which starts each cycle. The admin moves the ceiling up or down in the Admin center (MA-116), or from phase 18 in the admin's chat (§20.3): the admin only, behind the second check (MA-76, MA-102), with an audit row and a message to the admin in the chat; it takes effect on the next model call. Lowering it below what is already spent puts MyPaisa straight onto the step that implies, and the Admin center says which before Confirm. The chat can report spend (get_spend) but not change it.

The meter (MA-113). Every model call's cost is worked out from the API's own usage counts and the price table: input, output and thinking, cache writes and reads, web searches, Batches at half price. It is added to the cycle's SpendMonth row with an atomic add, by member, feature, agent, model and day, and written on the RunRecord as before. Before every call the agent reads that row, one item, and the step decides whether and how the call is made. The meter is reconciled weekly against the Console (§10).

The ladder (MA-113, MA-114):

Spent this cycleWhat happens
under 70%Normal.
70%One message to the owner: what is spent, on what, and the days left. Nothing changes.
85%Savers: the bull/bear debate is skipped, the Saturday review runs on Sonnet instead of Opus, "Why?" searches are limited to three a day, and the nightly sentiment scoring pauses (stances keep the last index). The owner is told.
95%Essentials only: chat answers on Haiku; the brief, the wrap and alerts go out as their plain templates with the engine's numbers; reading a factsheet or screenshot still works. The owner is told.
100%Claude stops until the cycle renews or the owner raises the ceiling. Every card goes out as its plain template, buttons and guided flows work as before, and a typed message gets one line saying free text is back on the renewal day.

Alerts are never dropped at any step: a template needs no model. Raising the ceiling moves MyPaisa back up the ladder at the next call.

Guards under the ladder, so one bad hour cannot spend a month:

  • Per member: no member spends more than their share of the ceiling; past it, that member's chat runs at the 95% step for the rest of the cycle.
  • Per day: no day spends more than three times the cycle's daily average (about $16 a day at a $160 ceiling); past it, the rest of the day runs at the 95% step.
  • Per run: at most eight model calls in one run; a loop that reaches it stops and says so.

The backstop is the Console. Each workspace's spend limit in the Claude Console is set by hand to its hard limit ($160 for prod and $40 for dev to start) and stops the key outright if the meter is ever wrong. The Admin center moves the ceiling anywhere from $10 to that hard limit; to go higher, raise the Console limit first and then type the new hard limit into the Admin center. The app holds no Console admin key, so it cannot change the Console itself.

One credit, two environments. The plan's $200 credit pays for both workspaces, and the two workspace limits ($160 and $40) add up to it, so neither environment can eat the other's share.

15.2 Caps and alerts, set by the admin in the Admin center (MA-137)

Page 7: the two caps, the meters, the alert router and where each alert goes; the agent team and the settings service of §20 sit above them. Open it in Lucid. Every threshold below is a setting the admin changes in the Admin center's Spend and Alerts pages (and, from phase 18, from the admin's chat, §20.3), behind the second check, each change an audit row and a message in the admin's chat.

Claude. The ceiling per environment, the ladder's steps (any ascending list; 70%, 85%, 95% and 100% by default), what each step does (§15.1), the member share, the daily guard, the calls per run, each agent's share (§20.7) and the eval caps (§23.7, on the Evals page). Hard stops behind them: the Console workspace limits, then the credit itself.

AWS and others. AWS has no true spending cap, so the app caps by acting:

SettingDefault
Monthly cap for AWS and paid data feeds, both environments£50
Alert steps50%, 80%, 100%, and when the month's forecast passes the cap
At 80%Dev's schedules pause
At 100%Dev pauses; prod's non-essential jobs (back-fills, retraining, challengers) pause; the brief, alerts and the chat never stop

The meter reads Cost Explorer once a day (month to date by service and by the env tag, and the month's forecast), about $0.01 a request, into AwsSpendMonth; AWS's own figures lag by up to a day, and the page says so. The actions are flags the jobs read, as /pause is, never changes to AWS resources, so Terraform stays the only thing that changes infrastructure. Behind them sits one AWS Budgets cost budget at a hard limit (£75 by default, set in dollars), made by Terraform and not from the app: it alerts on actual and forecast spend, and its action stops dev's schedules. Monitoring is free, and so are the first two action-enabled budgets; raising the hard limit is a pull request.

Alerts. One router sends every alert by a matrix the admin sets:

AlertAppPushEmail
Claude or AWS step reached, forecast over the capyesyesyes
Incident, an approval waiting (§20.3)yesyescritical only
Data feed stale, a job failedyesyesno
TestFlight build reaching day 60 of 90 (MA-167)yesyesyes (SNS from phase 0; app and push join here in phase 8)
Holding alerts (§11)members' own, as todaymembers' own, as todayno
  • App: the Alerts screen in the Admin center and its badge, for the admin only, for system alerts; members' holding alerts arrive in their own chat.
  • Push: to the admin's devices; critical alerts time-sensitive with sound, the rest passive and silent; no amounts on the lock screen.
  • Email: through Amazon SNS, a topic mypaisa-<env>-admin-alerts with the admin's address subscribed (put in SSM by hand, the subscription made by Terraform, confirmed once by clicking the link AWS sends). The first 1,000 email notifications a month are free, far more than this needs. Email carries system alerts only, never a holding's value, unless the admin turns that on.

Each channel has quiet hours that a critical alert may break through, every alert has a cooldown so it is not repeated, and non-urgent alerts can go to a daily digest instead.
