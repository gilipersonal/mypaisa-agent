6.6 Wealth maths (phases 2 to 7)

The same rule applies: code computes, Claude explains. The engine gains a wealth/ module, deterministic and tested against known answers. It starts from the old wealth tracker's tested TypeScript maths (xirr.ts, amortisation.ts with its prepay modes, forecast.ts, budget.ts and currency.ts; 646 tests), ported with their cases as golden fixtures (MA-105, phase 3), and extends it:

  • Net worth (phase 2; in GBP or INR from phase 3): the daily series by member, category, currency and country, materialised from the events (§4.8); the crossover of assets over debts.
  • Deposits and policies (phase 3, MA-94): accrual at the stated rate to maturity, with the PPF, EPF, NPS, SGB and LIC schedules; the maturity timeline (next twelve months, open-ended); what a maturing sum could do next.
  • Debts (phase 4, MA-204): the EMI, the schedule, the interest and principal split per year, the balance curve against the original plan, prepayment and preclosure what-ifs. A loan's terms are dated, and its events replay in order: a rate change, a floating-rate reset, the end of a fixed rate, a drawdown, a charge. A prepayment shortens the term or lowers the EMI, defaulting by country; a balance checkpoint starts a loan part-way through. Cards are revolving debt: the balance, the due date and the minimum come from statements; a payment is a transfer, and only interest and fees are spending. Every figure matches the fixtures to the penny.
  • XIRR (phase 5, MA-212) on the dated events of a holding, an account, a member or the household, with the current value as the closing flow: Newton's method with a bisection fallback, matched to a spreadsheet's XIRR on fixture ledgers to the penny, the old tracker's cases included. When it cannot be computed it returns nothing, with the reason, and the agent asks for past transactions or an estimate, marked as an estimate wherever the return shows (MA-215).
  • Budget (phase 6): plan against actual per category, income, EMIs, surplus and savings rate, per member and for the household (§12).
  • The Projection (phase 7), below.

Projection, not forecast. Forecast stays the market's word: a holding's next four weeks (§6.5, §19). The Projection is the household's net worth over the years (MA-191). ForecastAssumptions becomes ProjectionAssumptions, and each run is a ProjectionRun (§4.1).

The engine (wealth/projection.py, MA-219) runs month by month to a horizon (30 years by default), with a today's-money toggle. Its inputs:

  • Geography. Each record's country sets its inflation (consumer prices, with category overrides such as education and health), its wage-growth prior and its tax drag per wrapper. GBP/INR drifts each year by the gap between the two countries' inflation.
  • Income. Each member's current net salary. Its growth blends the member's own measured history (the yearly growth over past years' salaries) with the country's wage-growth prior, weighted by how many years of history there are.
  • Returns.
    • Contractual rates are used as they are: an FD, PPF, a bond's yield to maturity.
    • Market assets take their observed XIRR shrunk toward a long-run prior for their asset class and country, with weight w = (T/σ²)/(T/σ² + 1/τ²) on the observed figure and 1 − w on the prior, where T is the years of history, σ the asset's yearly volatility and τ how far the class's true return is believed to stray from its prior. Two years of equity history barely moves the prior; twenty years does.
    • With no history, the agent asks for past transactions (to compute the XIRR) or an estimate, which stays marked as an estimate.
    • Every rate can be corrected in place (§4.10).
  • Debts amortise to their real end dates, freeing their EMIs.
  • Also: maturities, the commitments, the budget's spend inflating by country, and the admin's plan items.
  • Surplus is allocated by a rule.
  • Constraints: a locked pension cannot cover a shortfall before it unlocks.

How it runs.

  • The deterministic base path ships first; a Monte Carlo fan (p10, p50, p90) follows, its seed stored.
  • Each run is a ProjectionRun: the inputs hash, the seed, the engine version, the percentiles and the milestones, so any curve can be reproduced. The nightly job runs it again, and so does a record closing or an assumption changing.
  • The engine ports the old tracker's tested forecast.ts, budget.ts and amortisation.ts with their cases as golden fixtures, then extends them: property, savings and pensions, the Monte Carlo, the priors and the country.
  • Priors (long-run returns, inflation and wage growth per country) are versioned settings with their sources, set by the admin (MA-221).

What the AI does (MA-223). Code produces every number; the model never does (rule 1).

  1. Proposes the assumption set from the data, with the reason for each figure.
  2. Answers what-ifs ("what if we buy a house in 2028?") by filling a typed override for run_projection (§8). A validator refuses any number not traceable to the member's words or a named preset; the result shows as an unsaved scenario card.
  3. Explains what drives the curve.
  4. Raises the trouble flags code computes, such as cash going negative when fees start.
  5. Notices milestones: a loan ending frees its EMI.

The admin's alone (MA-222). Plan items are future one-off or recurring income and spends, with a start, an end, a currency and a growth rate. They are created through the admin's chat (propose_plan_item) and corrected in place by the admin only, as are the saved assumptions. They need no second check, since they change a plan, not the records (§4.5). Members view the Projection and ask what-ifs, but save nothing.

The screen (§9.3, MA-224): the fan in GBP or INR; milestones on the line (loans ending, maturities, plan items); the assumptions panel, each figure with its source and editable by the admin; scenario chips; Ask.
