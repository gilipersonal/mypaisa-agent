6.6 Wealth maths (phases 2 to 7)

The same rule applies: code computes, Claude explains. The engine gains a wealth/ module, deterministic and tested against known answers. It starts from the old wealth tracker's tested TypeScript maths (xirr.ts, amortisation.ts with its prepay modes, forecast.ts, budget.ts and currency.ts; 646 tests), ported with their cases as golden fixtures (MA-105, phase 3), and extends it:

  • Net worth (phase 2; in GBP or INR from phase 3): the daily series by member, category, currency and country, materialised from the events (§4.8); the crossover of assets over debts.
  • Deposits and policies (phase 3, MA-94): accrual at the stated rate to maturity, with the PPF, EPF, NPS, SGB and LIC schedules; the maturity timeline (next twelve months, open-ended); what a maturing sum could do next.
  • Debts (phase 4, MA-204): the EMI, the schedule, the interest and principal split per year, the balance curve against the original plan, prepayment and preclosure what-ifs. A loan's terms are dated, and its events replay in order: a rate change, a floating-rate reset, the end of a fixed rate, a drawdown, a charge. A prepayment shortens the term or lowers the EMI, defaulting by country; a balance checkpoint starts a loan part-way through. Cards are revolving debt: the balance, the due date and the minimum come from statements; a payment is a transfer, and only interest and fees are spending. Every figure matches the fixtures to the penny.
  • XIRR (phase 5, MA-212) on the dated events of a holding, an account, a member or the household, with the current value as the closing flow: Newton's method with a bisection fallback, matched to a spreadsheet's XIRR on fixture ledgers to the penny, the old tracker's cases included. When it cannot be computed it returns nothing, with the reason, and the agent asks for past transactions or an estimate, marked as an estimate wherever the return shows (MA-215).
  • Budget (phase 6): plan against actual per category, income, EMIs, surplus and savings rate, per member and for the household (§12).
  • The Projection (phase 7), below.

Projection, not forecast. Forecast stays the market's word: a holding's next four weeks (§6.5, §19). The Projection is the household's net worth over the years (MA-191). ForecastAssumptions becomes ProjectionAssumptions, and each run is a ProjectionRun (§4.1).

The engine (wealth/projection.py, MA-219) runs month by month to a horizon (20 years by default; the screen offers 5, 10, 20 and 30), with a today's-money toggle, money in Decimal rounded half-even only where it is shown or stored. Every amount is take-home: the engine does no tax maths (Arun, 28 Oct 2026, MA-261). Its inputs:

  • Geography. Each record's country sets its inflation (consumer prices, with category overrides such as education and health) and its wage-growth prior. GBP/INR drifts each year by the gap between the two countries' inflation.
  • Income. Each member's current net salary. Its growth blends the member's own measured history (the yearly growth over past years' salaries) with the country's wage-growth prior, weighted by how many years of history there are.
  • Returns.
    • Contractual rates are used as they are: an FD, PPF, a bond's yield to maturity.
    • Market assets take their observed XIRR shrunk toward a long-run prior for their asset class and country, with weight w = (T/σ²)/(T/σ² + 1/τ²) on the observed figure and 1 − w on the prior, where T is the years of history, σ the asset's yearly volatility and τ how far the class's true return is believed to stray from its prior. Two years of equity history barely moves the prior; twenty years does.
    • With no history, the agent asks for past transactions (to compute the XIRR) or an estimate, which stays marked as an estimate.
    • Every rate can be corrected in place (§4.10).
  • Debts amortise to their real end dates, freeing their EMIs.
  • Also: maturities, the commitments, the budget's spend inflating by country, and the admin's plan items (below).
  • Surplus is allocated by a rule.
  • Constraints: a locked pension cannot cover a shortfall before it unlocks.

Plan items and their effects (MA-261). Each month the engine resolves the flows in one order: the commitments; then change_flow plan items (a salary, a SIP or a budget category set to, or changed by, an amount or a percentage from a date, the latest start winning for the same target); then new_flow plan items; then record_event plan items (a loan prepaid is re-amortised; one that covers the balance closes it and frees its EMI from the next month); then spend inflation by country and category; then the surplus rule. Growth steps up on each anniversary of a plan's start, fixed or at its category's prior (school fees, for instance). A one-off draws on funded_from; when none is named it is paid from the surplus and then cash in its currency first, and a pool running short is a flag, never silent borrowing. Each flow is kept in its own currency and converted at that month's projected GBP/INR rate.

What it puts out.

  • The net-worth path (deterministic, then the fan).
  • The budget forecast (MA-262): per month and per year, income by member, spend by category, EMIs, each plan item, the surplus, the savings rate and closing cash.
  • A trace for every figure: the flows that make it, so "why is 2031's surplus −£600?" is answered from code.
  • Its tests hold the ledger: every month, opening cash plus its flows equals closing cash; a closed loan has no EMI after it; a paused plan changes nothing.

How it runs.

  • The deterministic base path ships first; a Monte Carlo fan (p10, p50, p90) follows, its seed stored.
  • Each run is a ProjectionRun: the inputs hash (the records, the priors and every active plan item's version), the seed, the engine version, the percentiles and the milestones, so any curve can be reproduced. The nightly job runs it again, and so does a record closing or being corrected, a statement confirmed, a month closed, a plan item created, corrected, paused or ended, or an assumption changing; each door shows the new run on records.changed (§21.5).
  • The engine ports the old tracker's tested forecast.ts, budget.ts and amortisation.ts with their cases as golden fixtures, then extends them: property, savings and pensions, the Monte Carlo, the priors and the country.
  • Priors (long-run returns, inflation and wage growth per country) are versioned settings with their sources, set by the admin (MA-221).

What the AI does (MA-223). Code produces every number; the model never does (rule 1).

  1. Proposes the assumption set from the data, with the reason for each figure.
  2. Answers what-ifs ("what if we buy a house in 2028?") by filling a typed override for run_projection (§8). A validator refuses any number not traceable to the member's words or a named preset; the result shows as an unsaved scenario card.
  3. Explains what drives the curve, from the engine's trace.
  4. Raises the trouble flags code computes, such as cash going negative when fees start.
  5. Notices milestones: a loan ending frees its EMI.
  6. Turns the admin's words into a plan item (MA-222, MA-261): "£4,000 a month from Jan 2029 for my kid's schooling", "my salary goes to £8,000 a month from Sep 2028", "₹2 cr to close my SBI home loan". propose_plan_item returns what is missing, and the agent asks only that, at most three questions with chips (whose, until when, how it grows, paid from where); then an impact card from the engine (net worth at 5, 10 and 20 years, the surplus each year, the first month cash would go negative, new milestones) and one Confirm. Every number traces to the admin's words, a named prior or the engine.
  7. Rearranges the budget (MA-263). When a plan leaves cash below zero or the surplus below the admin's floor, code works out the options with a deterministic goal-seek (trim a category from a date, pause or reduce a SIP, move a plan's start, draw from a named pool, change the surplus rule); rearrange_budget returns them and the agent ranks and explains them, each as an unsaved scenario card. The admin's Confirm applies one, as Claude proposes a change and you accept it (Arun, 28 Oct 2026): change_flow plan items from their dates and, where it touches this month, the current month's budget plan, each with its audit row and Undo.

The admin's alone (MA-222). Plan items are future one-off or recurring income and spends, changes to existing flows and events on records, with a start, an end, a currency and a growth rate. They are created through the admin's chat (propose_plan_item) and corrected, paused or ended in place by the admin only, as are the saved assumptions and a rearrangement's Confirm. They need no second check, since they change a plan, not the records (§4.5). Each change writes its audit row with the admin's own words (source_text), the plan item's version before and after, and the Projection run before and after with the change it made to net worth at 20 years (MA-261). Members view the Projection, the plans and the Budget forecast, and ask what-ifs, but save nothing.

The screen (§9.3, MA-224): the fan in GBP or INR, opening at 20 years with 5, 10, 20 and 30 to choose; milestones on the line (loans ending, maturities, plan items); the assumptions panel, each figure with its source and editable by the admin; the Plans tab, each plan corrected or paused in place by the admin, with Add in chat, since nothing is created in place; the Budget forecast tab (MA-262), each year expandable to its months and every figure opening its flows; scenario chips; Ask.
