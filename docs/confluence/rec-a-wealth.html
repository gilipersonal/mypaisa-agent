6. Wealth maths: XIRR, CAGR, the budget and the Projection (phases 2 to 7)

Code computes, Claude explains (§1, rule 1). The wealth engine is the wealth/ module, deterministic and tested against known answers, and it works only from the household's actual numbers: the dated ledger, contractual rates, salaries, debts, commitments and plans. It predicts no market. It starts from the old wealth tracker's tested TypeScript maths (xirr.ts, amortisation.ts with its prepay modes, forecast.ts, budget.ts and currency.ts; 646 tests), ported with their cases as golden fixtures (MA-105, phase 3), and extends it:

  • Net worth (phase 2; in GBP, INR, USD or EUR from phase 3): the daily series by member, category, currency and country, materialised from the events (§4.8); the crossover of assets over debts.
  • Deposits and policies (phase 3, MA-94): accrual at the stated rate to maturity, with the PPF, EPF, NPS, SGB and LIC schedules; the maturity timeline (next twelve months, open-ended); what a maturing sum could do next.
  • Debts (phase 4, MA-204): the EMI, the schedule, the interest and principal split per year, the balance curve against the original plan, prepayment and preclosure what-ifs. A loan's terms are dated, and its events replay in order: a rate change, a floating-rate reset, the end of a fixed rate, a drawdown, a charge. A prepayment shortens the term or lowers the EMI, defaulting by country; a balance checkpoint starts a loan part-way through. Cards are revolving debt: the balance, the due date and the minimum come from statements; a payment is a transfer, and only interest and fees are spending. Every figure matches the fixtures to the penny.
  • XIRR (phase 5, MA-212) on the dated events of a holding, an account, a member or the household, with the current value as the closing flow: Newton's method with a bisection fallback, matched to a spreadsheet's XIRR on fixture ledgers to the penny, the old tracker's cases included. When it cannot be computed it returns nothing, with the reason, and the agent asks for past transactions or an estimate, marked as an estimate wherever the return shows (MA-215).
  • CAGR beside it (phase 5): the compound yearly growth between two dated values, (end ÷ start)^(1 ÷ years) − 1, for a unit price, an asset's value, a member's or the household's net worth. XIRR is the return on the money put in and taken out on their dates; CAGR is how fast a value grew, so each is labelled for what it measures, and both show their working under Technical.
  • Budget (phase 6): plan against actual per category, income, EMIs, surplus and savings rate, per member and for the household (§11).
  • The Projection (phase 7), below.
  • Goals, what changed and why, scenarios, the richer fan and tax-year facts (phases 5 to 9, §6.1 to §6.5), after it.

Two wealth forecasts, both from actual numbers (Arun, 29 Oct 2026, MA-275: "budget forecast will need to deterministic algo based on inputs and current circumstances of users"; and on 2 Nov 2026, MA-307: "forecast my wealth growth based on actual numbers like CAGR and XIRR"). The market forecast he set beside them on 29 Oct was retired by MA-307.

The Budget forecast and the Projection learn only through their inputs: the household's own closed months tune their assumptions on the admin's Confirm (§18.8), and the arithmetic stays the same. ForecastAssumptions becomes ProjectionAssumptions, and each run is a ProjectionRun (§4.1).

The engine (wealth/projection.py, MA-219) runs month by month to a horizon (20 years by default; the screen offers 5, 10, 20 and 30), with a today's-money toggle, money in Decimal rounded half-even only where it is shown or stored. Every amount is take-home: the engine does no tax maths (Arun, 28 Oct 2026, MA-261). Its inputs:

  • Geography. Each record's country sets its inflation (consumer prices, with category overrides such as education and health) and its wage-growth prior. Each currency's rate against GBP drifts each year by the gap between its country's inflation and the UK's.
  • Income. Each member's current net salary. Its growth blends the member's own measured history (the yearly growth over past years' salaries) with the country's wage-growth prior, weighted by how many years of history there are.
  • Returns.
    • Contractual rates are used as they are: an FD, PPF, a bond's yield to maturity.
    • Market assets take their observed XIRR shrunk toward a long-run prior for their asset class and country, with weight w = (T/σ²)/(T/σ² + 1/τ²) on the observed figure and 1 − w on the prior, where T is the years of history, σ the asset's yearly volatility and τ how far the class's true return is believed to stray from its prior. Two years of equity history barely moves the prior; twenty years does.
    • With no history, the agent asks for past transactions (to compute the XIRR) or an estimate, which stays marked as an estimate.
    • Every rate can be corrected in place (§4.10).
  • Debts amortise to their real end dates, freeing their EMIs.
  • Also: maturities, the commitments, the budget's spend inflating by country, and the admin's plan items (below).
  • Surplus stays as cash in its own currency, every month, on the base path and the fan alike (Arun, 3 Nov 2026: "All to cash"; §15). Nothing is swept into a holding or a loan; an admin who wants it invested says so as a plan item, and the goal solver (§6.1) and the scenario workspace (§6.3) test such a plan without saving it.
  • Constraints: a locked pension cannot cover a shortfall before it unlocks.

Plan items and their effects (MA-261). Each month the engine resolves the flows in one order: the commitments; then change_flow plan items (a salary, a SIP or a budget category set to, or changed by, an amount or a percentage from a date, the latest start winning for the same target); then new_flow plan items; then record_event plan items (a loan prepaid is re-amortised; one that covers the balance closes it and frees its EMI from the next month); then spend inflation by country and category; then the surplus, kept as cash in its currency. Growth steps up on each anniversary of a plan's start, fixed or at its category's prior (school fees, for instance). A one-off draws on funded_from; when none is named it is paid from the surplus and then cash in its currency first, and a pool running short is a flag, never silent borrowing. Each flow is kept in its own currency and converted at that month's projected rate for that currency.

What it puts out.

  • The net-worth path (deterministic, then the fan).
  • The budget forecast (MA-262): per month and per year, income by member, spend by category, EMIs, each plan item, the surplus, the savings rate and closing cash.
  • A trace for every figure: the flows that make it, so "why is 2031's surplus −£600?" is answered from code.
  • Its tests hold the ledger: every month, opening cash plus its flows equals closing cash; a closed loan has no EMI after it; a paused plan changes nothing.

How it runs.

  • The deterministic base path ships first; a Monte Carlo fan (p10, p50, p90) follows, its seed stored.
  • Each run is a ProjectionRun: the inputs hash (the records, the priors and every active plan item's version), the seed, the engine version, the percentiles and the milestones, so any curve can be reproduced. The nightly job runs it again, and so does a record closing or being corrected, a statement confirmed, a month closed, a plan item created, corrected, paused or ended, or an assumption changing; each door shows the new run on records.changed (§19.5).
  • The engine ports the old tracker's tested forecast.ts, budget.ts and amortisation.ts with their cases as golden fixtures, then extends them: property, savings and pensions, the Monte Carlo, the priors and the country.
  • Priors (long-run returns, inflation and wage growth per country) are versioned settings with their sources, set by an admin (MA-221).

What the AI does (MA-223). Code produces every number; the model never does (rule 1).

  1. Proposes the assumption set from the data, with the reason for each figure.
  2. Answers what-ifs ("what if we buy a house in 2028?") by filling a typed override for run_projection (§7). A validator refuses any number not traceable to the member's words or a named preset; the result shows as an unsaved scenario card.
  3. Explains what drives the curve, from the engine's trace.
  4. Raises the trouble flags code computes, such as cash going negative when fees start.
  5. Notices milestones: a loan ending frees its EMI.
  6. Turns the admin's words into a plan item (MA-222, MA-261): "£4,000 a month from Jan 2029 for my kid's schooling", "my salary goes to £8,000 a month from Sep 2028", "₹2 cr to close my SBI home loan". propose_plan_item returns what is missing, and the agent asks only that, at most three questions with chips (whose, until when, how it grows, paid from where); then an impact card from the engine (net worth at 5, 10 and 20 years, the surplus each year, the first month cash would go negative, new milestones) and one Confirm. Every number traces to the admin's words, a named prior or the engine.
  7. Rearranges the budget (MA-263). When a plan leaves cash below zero or the surplus below the admin's floor (a monthly surplus kept among the Projection's assumptions, £500 to start, the admin's alone, holding while anyone in the household earns a salary; in retirement only cash running out is flagged; Arun, 28 Oct 2026, MA-264), code works out the options with a deterministic goal-seek (trim a category from a date, pause or reduce a SIP, move a plan's start, draw from a named pool, change the surplus rule); rearrange_budget returns them and the agent ranks and explains them, each as an unsaved scenario card. The admin's Confirm applies one, as Claude proposes a change and you accept it (Arun, 28 Oct 2026): change_flow plan items from their dates and, where it touches this month, the current month's budget plan, each with its audit row and Undo.

An admin's alone (MA-222, MA-308). Plan items are future one-off or recurring income and spends, changes to existing flows and events on records, with a start, an end, a currency and a growth rate. They are created through an admin's chat (propose_plan_item), corrected, paused or ended in the chat or in place, and deleted only in the chat (MA-309), by an admin only, as are the saved assumptions and a rearrangement's Confirm. They need no second check, since they change a plan, not the records (§4.5). Each change writes its audit row with the admin's own words (source_text), the plan item's version before and after, and the Projection run before and after with the change it made to net worth at 20 years (MA-261). Members view the Projection, the plans and the Budget forecast, and ask what-ifs, but save nothing.

The screen (§8.3, MA-224): the fan in GBP, INR, USD or EUR, opening at 20 years with 5, 10, 20 and 30 to choose; milestones on the line (loans ending, maturities, plan items); the assumptions panel (the admin's floor among them), each figure with its source and editable by the admin; the Plans tab, each plan on a timeline, corrected or paused in place by the admin, with Add in chat, since nothing is created in place; the Budget forecast tab (MA-262), each year's leanest month against the floor first, each year expandable to its months and every figure opening its flows; scenario chips; Ask. MA-264 drew it; Arun picked the Budget forecast's 1a, the chat's 2a and the Plans tab's 3a on 28 Oct 2026 (docs/design/reviews/MA-264/decision.md).

6.1 Goals and the goal solver (phase 7, MA-314)

Arun, 2 Nov 2026, after the agentic review: "I need this to be super intelligent agentic ai based wealth tracker and forecasting engine, interactive chat based conversations etc."; he picked the review's P1 and P2 whole ("Need all of the above"). Sections 6.1 to 6.5, 7.1, 7.2, 10.1, the fan's grading in §18.8 and the reasoning cases in §21.3 are those picks.

A goal is a target the household is saving towards: "£40,000 for a car deposit by Sep 2028", "₹1.5 cr for education by 2035". It is a Goal (§4.1): a target in its own currency, a date, the pools it is funded from (records such as a savings account or an ISA, or a budget category), a priority and an owner, the household or one member. Until now such a sentence could only be a memory note with no figure (§22.2) or a plan item, which is a flow, not a target.

The solver (wealth/goals.py) works on the deterministic path (§6), so every figure it returns comes from the engine and can be reproduced:

  1. Where it stands. The funded-from pools' value today against the target, in the goal's currency at the day's ECB rate.
  2. When, at today's saving. The first month the pools' projected value reaches the target on the base path, or "not by the horizon".
  3. What it would take. The monthly saving into the named pool, from next month to by, that makes the pool meet the target on its date: a bisection on a change_flow amount over the base path, to the pound, bounded below by zero and above by what keeps the surplus over the admin's floor (§6, item 7). If no amount inside that bound reaches it, the solver says so and returns the gap and the leanest month.
  4. The other levers, each as an option with its figures, never as a call (§1, rule 2): a later date (the date reached at today's saving), a lump sum from a named pool, a category trimmed from a date (the goal-seek of rearrange_budget). The agent ranks and explains them; an admin's Confirm turns one into a plan item.
  5. The chance, from phase 7's fan (§6.4, MA-318): the share of the run's paths whose pools are at or above the target on by, stored on the ProjectionRun as goal_odds.

Several goals that draw on the same pool are solved in priority order: the saving the first needs is fixed in the path before the second is solved, so two goals never count the same pound. A goal's glide path is the pool's base path to by, stored with the goal when it is saved or changed; the insight job (§10.1) notices a pool falling off it, and the tuner grades it each month (§18.8).

Who does what. Every member asks: solve_goal reads (§7.1). A goal is saved, corrected, paused or ended by an admin alone, through propose_change(entity: goal, …) and Confirm, with its audit row and the admin's own words, as a plan item is (§4.5, §4.10); it changes a plan, not the records, so it needs no second check. Goals show as milestones on the Projection's line and as a Goals list on the Projection screen (drawn with /design-first before MA-314 starts). A goal reached is marked reached by code at the month close, with a line in the admins' digest.

6.2 What changed and why: the attribution engine (phase 5, its Overview part in phase 9; MA-315)

"Computes what moved and why" (the opening of this document) and the Overview's "where growth comes from" (§11) now have their mechanism. The engine's trace explains a future figure; attribution explains a past change.

The Attribution view (wealth/attribution.py), between any two dates (a month by default), for the household, a member, a record, a class or a country, splits the change in net worth into parts that add up to it:

PartWhat it is
SavedTake-home income less spending, interest and fees, from the budget's actuals and the commitments (§4.9, §11): what the household kept
MarketsThe price change on market-valued records, in their own currency, translated at the end rate
CurrencyThe FX change on every record not in the reporting currency: an INR deposit's value in pounds moving with GBP/INR
AccrualInterest and growth at contract rates on deposits, policies and pensions (§6, phase 3)
RevaluationsA new valuation of a property or another marked asset, from its event
CorrectionsAn admin's correction (§4.10) or a late statement that changes a past figure
OtherRecords opened, closed, deleted or restored, and any residual, shown with its records, never hidden

A second split says where the saving went: into cash, into investments, into a debt's principal. Paying down a loan moves money from cash to the debt, so it does not change net worth; it is shown where the saving went, not as a cause of the change.

It is worked out day by day from the daily views (§4.8) and summed, with each day's flows at that day's rate, so the parts add up to the change to the penny and the price and FX parts never overlap. Each part carries its records and their events, so any figure opens what makes it. It is a view: rebuilt from the events and the prices like the others, never edited. It needs prices and FX, so it is built in phase 5 (MA-315); the Overview's card reads it when MA-95 completes the Overview in phase 9.

The tool explain_change(scope, from, to) returns the parts, the largest records behind each and their trace ids (§7.1); Claude chooses the words: "Net worth rose £3,100 in October: £1,900 saved, £1,600 from markets, −£450 from the rupee on the Indian deposits, and £50 of other changes" (illustrative figures).

The claim check. The figure check (§21.2) proves a reply's numbers; it cannot prove its causes. So a reply that explains a change (from explain_change, the engine's trace or compare_scenarios) carries its claims[] beside the text: each sentence that states a cause names the part or trace id it rests on. Code checks that each id was returned by a tool in this run, that the sentence's figure and direction match it (a "fell" on a positive part fails), and that no sentence with a causal word ("because", "due to", "driven by", "thanks to", "mainly", "after") goes without one. A miss regenerates once, then the plain template goes, as for a figure. The judge for "causal claims faithful to the trace or the attribution" (§21.3) watches what the words check cannot.

6.3 The scenario workspace (phase 7, MA-317)

"Compare: a house in 2028, a house in 2030, no house." One run_projection call answers one what-if, so a three-way comparison used to cost three calls of the run's eight (§14.1) and three cards. The workspace makes it one.

  • A Scenario (§4.1) is a named set of typed overrides, the same shape as run_projection's, on a base run. It is unsaved (a draft) until an admin saves it by name with Confirm (propose_change(entity: scenario, …)), with its audit row; saved scenarios run again with each nightly Projection, so their figures are always current, at no Claude cost.
  • compare_scenarios(scenarios[≤4]) (§7.1) runs the base and up to four scenarios in one call, on one inputs hash and seed, and returns them side by side: net worth at 5, 10 and 20 years, the leanest month and its cash, the first month cash goes negative, each goal's date and chance (§6.1), the milestones, and each one's difference from the base. One card shows them; every figure comes from the engine.
  • Promoted with one Confirm. An admin turns a scenario into plan items: propose_plan_item drafts one per override, shown together on one card with the impact; one Confirm writes each with its own audit row, and the scenario is marked promoted.
  • The validator holds (§6, item 2): every number in an override traces to the member's words or a named preset.
  • Who saves. Only an admin saves, names and promotes a scenario (Arun, 3 Nov 2026: "Admins only"; §15). Every member may ask a what-if or a comparison in the chat and see its result, which is never saved.

6.4 A richer Monte Carlo, and what moves the result most (phase 7, MA-318)

The deterministic path is the Budget forecast and the Projection's spine; the fan around it (phase 7, after the path, §6 "How it runs") used to vary only market returns. For a household in the UK and India the larger risks are inflation in each country and the rupee against the pound, and they move together. The fan now draws them jointly. Nothing here predicts a market: every range comes from stated priors, labelled as such, and the fan is graded against what happens (§18.8).

  • Joint draws, each year of each path: a return for each asset class and country the household holds, CPI for the UK and India, and the GBP/INR rate. They are correlated through a matrix and fat-tailed: a multivariate Student-t with a low degrees-of-freedom prior, scaled so each variable keeps its prior's volatility. GBP/INR drifts by the inflation gap, as on the base path, plus its own volatility.
  • Inflation flows through. Each path's CPI drives its spend by country and category (with each category's spread over CPI) and its salary growth, so a high-inflation path is lean as well as low.
  • Contractual rates stay as given: an FD, PPF, a bond's yield to maturity, a loan's dated terms and resets. A floating rate follows its stated terms, not a draw.
  • Sequence risk. In a year whose shortfall is met from market pools (retirement, a large one-off), the path sells at that year's drawn return, so a bad run early in a drawdown shows. The run stores short_odds, the chance cash or a pool runs out before the horizon.
  • Priors, versioned with their sources (MA-221): each class's long-run return and volatility, each country's CPI mean, volatility and persistence, GBP/INR's volatility, the correlation matrix and the tail weight, each a setting with its source and the window it was measured on, set by an admin. Their versions are in the run's inputs hash, with the seed and the engine version, so a run is reproduced to the penny.
  • Size. About 5,000 paths of monthly steps to the horizon, vectorised in NumPy in the worker, inside Lambda's free tier: no Claude cost.

The sensitivity table. For each input that can move the result (each class's return prior, each country's CPI, GBP/INR's drift, each member's salary growth, the largest spend categories, each plan item's amount), code moves it by a stated step (one standard deviation of its prior, or 10% of an amount) up and down on the deterministic path, and records the change in net worth at the horizon and in the leanest month. The five that move it most are drawn as a tornado on the Projection screen and listed under Technical; the table is stored on the run (sensitivity[]), so "what matters most for 2045?" is answered from code. Plan with me reads it (§7.2).

6.5 Tax-year facts and dates (phase 9; India in phase 14; MA-320)

Facts and dates, never advice: the engine still does no tax maths and every amount stays take-home (MA-261), and tax advice is still refused (§21.2). What MyPaisa can say is what a government page states and what the household's own ledger shows: "£14,200 of this year's ISA allowance used; the tax year ends on 5 April".

  • Facts as versioned settings (§18.2), each with its source: the page (gov.uk, and from phase 14 the Income Tax Department of India), the date it was read and the dates it applies from and to. The UK's: the tax year (6 April to 5 April), the ISA allowance and its parts, the pension annual allowance, the Self Assessment deadline. India's, in phase 14 with MA-153: the financial year (1 April to 31 March), the return's due date, and section 80C's limit, only for a member flagged as filing in India under the old regime. Each value is checked against its source when the story starts [verify]; a fact past its end date is flagged on the Admin center's Settings, and not used, until an admin enters the new one.
  • What the ledger shows, by code: each member's contributions into their ISA-type records in the current tax year, and the room left; the pension contributions recorded; from phase 14, the 80C-eligible contributions in the financial year. "Used" means what the ledger holds, and the card says so when records are missing.
  • get_calendar(window) (§7.1) returns the dates in a window: tax-year ends and deadlines, maturities, fixed-rate ends and rate resets, commitments due and goal dates, with each allowance used and left in the asker's scope (§4.5).
  • The insight job's deadline detector (§10.1): an allowance with room left within 30 days of its year's end, said as a fact ("£5,800 of the ISA allowance is unused; the tax year ends on 5 April"), never "you should top it up".
  • Where the line is (§21.2): what to claim, which regime to choose, how to pay less tax, or whether to move money into a wrapper are tax advice and get a plain refusal; a stated allowance, its source and what the ledger shows used are facts and are answered.
