15 · Money in any currency, checked against the holding

Every holding has one currency: the one it is bought and valued in, as the platform shows it, confirmed once on the card that added it. Type money in any currency and MyPaisa looks up that holding's currency and checks the amount against it. Rupees typed for a pound holding are never saved as typed: MyPaisa says why and asks again, in pounds.

Trigger "I put ₹20,000 into Balanced" Phase 2 (the check) Phase 3 (the ECB-rate estimate) Phase 5 (rupee holdings)

📌 What the member types, and what MyPaisa does

"I put 200 into Balanced"

No currency named: the holding's is assumed, and the Confirm card says "£200, in pounds as Balanced is".

"I put £200 into Balanced"

The same currency: the usual Confirm card of flow 4.

"I put ₹20,000 into Balanced"

Saves nothing yet. Balanced is held in pounds, so MyPaisa asks How many pounds arrived? Type them or attach the platform's screenshot; or tap the estimate, its figure on the button (About £177.78 · ECB estimate); or put it in a rupee holding by name (Nifty 50 instead, phase 5); or Cancel.

"1.5 lakh into my Nifty 50 index fund"

Phase 5: an INR holding, so ₹1,50,000 with no question. Pounds typed against it get the same correction the other way.

"Which currency is Balanced in?"

"Pounds (GBP), from the factsheet you sent on 3 March."

A "$" on its own

US dollars, unless the member holds another dollar; then MyPaisa asks which.

👀 What the member sees

  • The question, in pounds. Nothing saved. The estimate shows its figure before you choose it; a rupee holding is offered by name only when one is held.
  • The ECB estimate. One tap, the engine's conversion, its rate and date on the card, marked an estimate; Confirm is still its own tap.
  • The pounds that arrived. Typed in answer; the question drops its buttons. Your figure is recorded; the rupees and the rate the engine works out from them stay beside it.
  • The other way. Lakh read by code, no question for rupees; pounds against a rupee holding get the same question, in rupees.

🛡️ Safeguards on this path

One currency per holding

Position.ccy, read from the factsheet or screenshot and shown on the Confirm card that added it: confirmed once, never guessed again. A fund's own base currency (often USD) can differ; the position's is the one that counts.

Reading amounts is code

Symbols, the codes of every currency the ECB publishes, the words, lakh and crore. The figure is always the member's own, echoed back for Confirm.

A converted figure is never Claude's

Either the pounds that arrived, or the engine's estimate at the ECB rate of the day, its figure on the button before you choose it, then on the card with the rate and its date, marked an estimate.

The original kept

What was typed and how it became the recorded figure stay on the Transaction (fx_source as credited, ECB or statement). The audit row shows both; a screenshot or statement behind it is its document_id.

Every pair, both ways, every door

A typed message, the guided flow, a screenshot, a CAS or broker line. Confirm before anything is saved.

Privacy mode

No amount and no rate on the card, only words, currencies and dates, so nothing on it leads to a figure. Show amounts reveals one card.

16 · Importing the existing Indian portfolio, once

The household's Indian mutual funds, shares and ETFs arrive once, with their whole history, from the statements India already produces. Each file is kept first; code parses it; every holding is matched by ISIN; a dry run shows everything; one Confirm, with the second check, writes it all. Anything that does not match is listed, never guessed.

Trigger you attach a CAS statement or a broker export in the chat Phase 19 (IN2) Needs IN1 and file keeping

📌 The files to send

Mutual funds, every fund house

The detailed Consolidated Account Statement (CAS) PDF from CAMS, KFintech or MF Central: every folio and scheme, the units, and every purchase, SIP, switch and redemption since inception, so cost and XIRR are right from day one.

Shares, ETFs and funds in demat

The NSDL or CDSL CAS, or the broker's holdings and tradebook export (for example Zerodha's tradebook CSV): holdings and quantities by ISIN, and the trades from the tradebook.

Anything else

A screenshot or a typed line, one holding at a time, as for the UK funds in flow 3.

Kept before it is read

Every file goes to S3 first with a Document row and an audit row, and is never deleted after reading.

Parsed by code

Matched by ISIN, never guessed

A fund to its AMFI scheme code, a share or ETF to its .NS or .BO listing; anything unmatched is listed.

A dry run, one Confirm, the second check

17 · The household in INR and GBP

Indian holdings sit in Indian accounts, in rupees, with the pound value beside every figure. The household total stays in the base currency, pounds, with the rupee view one tap away. Privacy mode hides rupees as it hides pounds; a suggestion names only what suits its account; and a holding the UK may tax differently is flagged in plain words.

Trigger Home in the app or the web app, a holding's card or its screen, once Indian accounts exist Phase 19 (IN3) Designed first

📌 The accounts, and how a figure is kept

Indian accounts

demat, mf_folio, nre and nro, and ppf, nps or elss if one is held; each with country IN and currency INR, owned by one member or two.

Stored as both

Each valuation keeps the native amount and its GBP equivalent on the day: price and price_ccy, fx_to_gbp, value_gbp.

Read in four currencies

Each member reads figures in GBP, INR, USD or EUR. The household total is in the base currency, GBP, with the other three one tap away.

Never tax advice

For a UK resident many Indian funds are not UK "reporting funds", so gains may be taxed differently. MyPaisa flags those holdings in plain words and says to check with a tax adviser.

🛡️ Safeguards on this path

Both figures, from the record

Rupees with pounds beside them, both from the stored valuation, never worked out by Claude.

Privacy mode

Hides rupees as it hides pounds, both on a two-currency line, on every screen and in every notification.

Wrapper-aware, never a trade

"ISA-safe" becomes "wrapper-aware": a suggestion names only actions that suit the account it is about, and the app still never trades.

Plain words, never tax advice

A possible non-reporting fund is flagged, with "check with a tax adviser".

One write service

The screens create nothing: anything new goes through the chat, the currency check, Confirm and an audit row, and a figure corrected in place goes through the same service.

18 · India in the daily rhythm

India closes while the UK is at work, and its fund prices arrive in the UK evening. Every schedule stays in Europe/London: the Indian jobs run on the work queue after those times, and the 07:15 brief carries India's previous day beside the UK's.

Trigger EventBridge Scheduler, Europe/London, after the Indian close and after AMFI's NAVs Phase 19 (IN1 prices, IN4 engine)

📌 The Indian day, on the UK clock

10:00 or 11:00NSE and BSE close at 15:30 IST: 10:00 UK time in winter, 11:00 in British Summer Time. NSE's holiday calendar says which days trade.

After the closeIngest for Indian shares and ETFs (in_listed): yfinance .NS, then .BO, then the exchanges' bhavcopy, stale last.

UK eveningAMFI's NAVs for every scheme are out. Ingest for Indian funds (in_mutual_fund): AMFI's file, then mfapi.in or a screenshot, stale last.

07:10The engine has scored every holding; the Indian ones read the India basket in their macro pillar.

07:15The brief carries India's previous day, in rupees with pounds beside them.

🛤️ The path

#LanesWhat happens
1AWSAfter the 15:30 IST close on a trading day (from NSE's holiday calendar), EventBridge Scheduler (Europe/London) checks the /pause flag, then puts one ingest job per Indian share or ETF on the SQS work queue
2AWS to External dataworker λ runs the in_listed chain: yfinance .NS, then .BO, then the exchanges' bhavcopy end-of-day file
3External data to AWSDaily candles in rupees, with their source; raw answers kept in S3
4AWS to External dataIn the UK evening, one job per Indian fund: AMFI's daily NAV file, matched by scheme code and ISIN, with mfapi.in behind it
5External data to AWSOne NAV a day per scheme, no candles, like a UK OEIC
6AWSThe ECB reference rate includes INR, with the RBI/FBIL rate behind it: each Indian Valuation is stored in rupees and in pounds on the day
7AWS to External dataNews jobs read Economic Times, Mint and Business Standard by RSS, NSE company announcements and GDELT's India coverage
8AWS to ClaudeHaiku tags each story through Batches: the companies, sectors and central bank it names, its event type
9Claude to AWSThe tags. Code matches each story to the holdings through exposure maps from the fund houses' monthly disclosures
10AWS07:10 the engine scores every holding. For Indian holdings the macro pillar reads the India basket: the Nifty 50 against Indian government bonds, India VIX, the rupee against the dollar, foreign portfolio investor flows and the RBI's policy rate
11AWS to App07:15 the brief, India's previous day included, through the outbox and sender λ; a push to a closed app, with no amount on the lock screen
✎NoteA source that answers nothing is skipped and the chain falls through to stale: labelled, left out of scoring, as in flow 5. The calendars add the RBI's policy dates, India's inflation releases, NSE holidays and results season. The Forecaster (phase 17) takes the Indian holdings on the same terms: nothing is shown until its gates are passed.

👀 What the member sees

  • The owner's brief. The UK lines in pounds, India's previous day in rupees with the total in pounds.
  • Labelled, not skipped. A late Indian price goes out marked stale; a holiday is known from the calendar.

🛡️ Safeguards on this path

One clock

Every schedule in Europe/London; the Indian jobs run after the 15:30 IST close and after AMFI's NAVs.

Free sources first

AMFI, mfapi.in, yfinance, the bhavcopy. A paid feed (Twelve Data's Grow plan, Zerodha Kite Connect) only on the admin's approval, inside the £50 cap.

Stale is labelled

Each Indian chain falls through to stale: the previous close, labelled and kept out of scoring.

The exchange's own calendar

NSE's trading holidays come from the exchange's calendar.

Retries and dead-letter queues

Each Indian job is a work-queue job: five tries with backoff, then its dead-letter queue and an alarm.

Numbers from code

Haiku tags the stories; code matches and scores them. News alone never raises a red alert.
