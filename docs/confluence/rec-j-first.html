📟 How a layman uses the bot, scene by scene

Eight journeys through the Telegram bot, played on a phone. Each one shows what you tap, what comes back, and what happens behind it. Nothing technical appears unless you ask; when you do, it is one tap away. These are mock-ups of the design; nothing is built yet.

Kept for reference. Eight user journeys on a phone frame, in the Nocturne look, from 23 Oct 2026 (journeys.html). Superseded by the Agent Journey (its twenty-four flows); the Telegram bot they show was superseded by the iOS app (MA-161), and the web app was kept beside it.

📱 First open

Three questions, then you are set up · at 19:02

  1. You send /start You open the chat and press Start. Telegram sends the update to the webhook, which checks it is you and hands it to the agent.
  2. MyPaisa: Hello Arun. I keep an eye on what you hold and tell you what matters. The setup is three cards. No forms, no app to install. The agent writes a preferences record as you go.
  3. You tap £ GBP A tap is a callback query; it travels the same path as a message. GBP is saved.
  4. MyPaisa: 2 of 3 · What do you hold? Any of the three ways works. The next journey shows the factsheet path.
  5. You tap Skip for now Skipping is fine; the bot works with whatever you give it.
  6. MyPaisa: 3 of 3 · How often should I speak? The ping budget, quiet hours and alert thresholds are set here and changeable later by just saying so.
  7. You tap Sounds right Preferences saved.
  8. MyPaisa: Done. The four buttons below are all you need: Portfolio, Today, Ask, Settings. Your first brief arrives tomorrow at 07:15. The reply keyboard appears. It stays at the bottom of the chat from now on. Commands exist (/add, /why, /pause) but you never have to remember them.

📱 Add a holding

A factsheet, a screenshot, two taps · at 19:06

  1. You send iShares Automation & Robotics UCITS ETF factsheet.pdf You share the factsheet from the Monzo app. Telegram stores it; the agent downloads it into a private bucket.
  2. MyPaisa: Reading the factsheet… Claude on Bedrock reads the PDF with a fixed schema: name, ISIN, charge, allocation, top holdings, benchmark, currency. Each figure comes back with the line it was read from and a confidence.
  3. MyPaisa: Here is what I read. Tap Confirm if it looks right. The agent resolved the ISIN to two London lines and guessed GBP. Amber means "I am not sure"; a tap on Edit lets you change it. Nothing is saved yet.
  4. You tap Confirm Only now is the Instrument written to the registry, with the document and its extracted facts.
  5. MyPaisa: Added. How much do you hold? Send a screenshot of your Monzo holdings screen, or type an amount. The position is separate from the instrument: the same ETF could sit in an ISA and a general account.
  6. You send a screenshot. Claude reads the values on it as an image. The agent matches "Automation & Robotics" to the holding it just created.
  7. MyPaisa: From the screenshot, 23 Oct: Units are derived from the value and the last close. The account question is asked because the factsheet cannot know it.
  8. You tap Confirm Position, Valuation (source: your screenshot) and a Transaction are written. The screenshot is deleted unless you say keep.
  9. MyPaisa: 📊 Portfolio · 23 Oct · £31,280 Done in about a minute and two taps. The same path adds a US share, a gold ETC or another fund; a known kind needs no code.

📱 Morning brief

Silent, six lines, detail on tap · at 07:15

  1. Mon 26 Oct · 07:15 07:15, Europe/London. Overnight the ingest fetched the closes, the fund NAV and the ECB rates; the engine ran its self-tests and computed the pillars, flags and ranges.
  2. MyPaisa: 🌅 Monday brief · £31,410 ▲0.4% · month ▲2.1% Sonnet 5.5 wrote the words from the engine's JSON. Every number came from a tool; the validator checks that before sending. Silent push: no sound.
  3. You tap Technical ▾ You want the numbers. The card edits in place; a rich message opens its collapsed section without a round trip.
  4. The technical section is the engine's scorecard: pillars from −2 to +2, flags, volatility percentiles, the 1-month ranges, and which data source answered. Tap any term for a one-line glossary.

📱 Ask why

A question in plain words, an answer with sources · at 13:12

  1. You send why did my silver drop so much this week? You ask in your own words. The webhook acks in 100 ms; the agent shows "typing…" while it works.
  2. MyPaisa: Looking at it… Claude calls get_holding("silver"), get_signals("silver") and get_macro. It cannot invent a number; it can only ask the tools.
  3. MyPaisa: Silver is down 6.2% this week, 9% from its high. The chart is a PNG rendered by code in the house style. The local-versus-FX split is the engine's; the sentence is Claude's.
  4. You tap Why the dollar? Follow-up. Chat memory holds the last turns and their tool results.
  5. MyPaisa: Two things, from the news (3 sources): Brave Search results arrive marked untrusted; they can explain, never alter a score. Links open in the browser.

📱 Should I add?

A stance with a confidence you can check · at 20:31

  1. You send should I put £200 into the Nasdaq one this month? The question the whole system exists for.
  2. MyPaisa: Checking the signals and your track record… get_signals, get_macro, get_events, get_stances. For a stance above 70% the agent also runs a short bull-versus-bear debate with a judge.
  3. MyPaisa: iShares NASDAQ 100 · stance ADD · confidence 64% The stance and its confidence are computed: pillar agreement, how long the state has held, data quality, and how calls in this state did before. Below 55% the card would say "no clear view".
  4. You tap Why 64%? Confidence is explainable, because it is arithmetic.
  5. MyPaisa: How 64% is made Every Saturday the reliability table checks that 64% means 64%. The mapping only changes after eight weeks of evidence, and the change is logged.
  6. You tap I acted You act in the Monzo app, then tell the bot. It cannot trade; it records the decision so the review can compare what you did with what the signals said.
  7. MyPaisa: Logged: £200 into NASDAQ 100 on Thu 29 Oct. I'll pick it up from your next screenshot or you can type the units. The decision log is part of the scorecard. Honest grading needs both what the bot said and what you did.

The one message a week that makes a sound · at 21:40

  2. MyPaisa: 🔴 NASDAQ 100 is 8.4% below its high Haiku checked this was not a repeat of something you were told this week; Sonnet wrote it. Sound on, for the first time this week.

  4. MyPaisa: Snoozed loud alerts on NASDAQ 100 until Wed 4 Nov. Briefs will still mention it. The alert state machine lives in code: fire → cooldown → re-arm. Claude never decides whether a rule fired, only whether it is worth saying.

  2. MyPaisa: 📒 Week 44 · £31,090 ▼0.6% this week · +2.4% this month The review is the only place the bot grades itself in public. The go-live gate for weekday stances is checked in code, not by the model.
  3. You tap Open dashboard The Mini App opens inside Telegram. A static page on CloudFront; it shows data only after validating Telegram's signed proof that it is you.
  4. Interactive charts (pinch, scroll), the overlap map, the scorecard. Read-only: every change still happens in the chat with a Confirm tap, so there is one place where state changes.
  5. Close it and you are back in the chat.

🗓️ Rules every journey keeps

Plain on top, detail on tap

Every card reads in two lines. "Technical ▾" opens the numbers; a glossary sits behind each term.

Nothing saved without Confirm

A misread factsheet or a mistyped amount cannot quietly change your records.

Numbers from code, words from Claude

Every figure in a message came out of a tool; Claude only chooses what to say.

A view, not advice; never "sell everything"

Stances are ADD, HOLD, PAUSE, TRIM or REBALANCE, with a confidence that is checked every Saturday.

It cannot trade

No broker login exists. You act in the Monzo app and tell the bot "I acted".

Silence is a feature

Cooldowns, a weekly budget of two loud alerts, quiet hours, and a filter that drops repeats.
