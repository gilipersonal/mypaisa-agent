Arun, 24 Oct 2026 (MA-117): "My target is to minimize loss and grow money over time steadily ... another agent which will give really predictive recommendation side by side with the one you are building ... I want this view of web dashboard as well as integration with my Telegram app."

The rules engine of §6 stays as it is: transparent, fixed rules that a person can check by hand. The Forecaster is a second engine beside it that learns from ten years of history, gives its own view of each holding, and is graded on the same track record. It never replaces the rules engine and never trades. (It is not the Projection of §6.6, the household's net worth over the years.)

19.1 What can and cannot be forecast

CanCannot
How rough the next few weeks will be, and the chance of a deep fall: calm and rough spells cluster, so risk is far more forecastable than directionTomorrow's up or down: short-term moves are mostly noise, and a tool claiming 80 to 90% accuracy has learned its past by heart
The market's mood (calm and rising, choppy, stressed, falling)Rare shocks: wars and pandemics have too few past cases for confident numbers
What usually followed days like today, and how a holding moved after past events of a kindCandles or opening prices for OEICs: they price once a day and deal at the next day's price
A modest, honest edge on direction over weeks, not daysAnything faster than the household can act on

So the horizon is four weeks (20 trading days), the first aim is avoiding deep falls, and direction is the last and least trusted piece.

When EODHD is taken: only when it has to be (Arun, 24 Oct 2026). Both engines run on yfinance, Twelve Data's free tier and free websites where their terms allow. EODHD is switched on only when the data steward (§20.1) shows that no free source can serve a named holding (or its ten-year history) and the admin approves the request, which shows the evidence, the £19.99 a month and the headroom in the £50 line for AWS and others. Before it asks, it tries EODHD's free key on that holding and records what comes back. A paid feed not needed for 60 days is proposed for cancellation. Share-level fundamentals (£59.99 a month, or £99.99 for the All-in-One plan) only if shares are added and their cards are wanted.

Not chosen, with the reason: Morningstar and FE fundinfo (the industry's UK fund data, sold on business licences); Twelve Data Grow ($79 a month; UK funds only on Pro at $229) while EODHD covers both; Alpha Vantage premium (from $49.99) and Marketaux (about $29) for news sentiment, while Claude Haiku tags the news (§19.3), with Marketaux the fallback if that tagging falls short; EODHD itself, until a free source fails (above).

Shares come from another platform. Monzo offers only BlackRock funds and ETFs, gold and silver among them, and no individual shares; a share would be held on another platform, in an Account with its own institution, and reaches MyPaisa the same way (a statement, a screenshot or a typed line).

Every row carries the date it became known, and revisions are added rather than overwritten, so a backtest of 2018 sees only what was known in 2018. A daily refresh runs after the close; a gap or a stale feed is an alarm in the chat. Each source's terms are recorded; the use is personal.

19.3 Matching news and events to the holdings (F1, F4; MA-119, MA-122)

Every holding has an exposure map, kept on the instrument's exposure (§4) and refreshed on its own clock:

KindWhat it is exposed toRefreshed
OEIC or ETFIts top holdings, countries, sectors and currencies, from the factsheet and the holdings file (look-through, §5.2)Monthly; iShares files daily
ShareThe company, its sector, its main markets, its main suppliers and rivalsEach quarter's results
Commodity ETCIts drivers. Gold: real yields, the dollar, central-bank buying, geopolitical stress. Silver: gold's drivers and industrial demand. Oil: OPEC+ decisions, stockpiles, supply from the Middle East and Russia, world growthA fixed list, reviewed yearly
CashIts interest rate and currencyWith the rate

Each day's stories are tagged. The news of §19.2 goes to Claude Haiku through Batches, which tags each story with the companies, countries, sectors, commodities and central banks it names, its event type (§19.4) and the direction it points. The tags are checked weekly on a labelled sample.

Relevance is worked out in code: a story's weight on a holding is the holding's exposure to the entities the story names. A chip-export story reaches a NASDAQ fund through its chipmakers' weight; an OPEC+ cut reaches an oil holding through its first driver; a surprise from the Fed reaches gold through real yields and the dollar. Stories under a floor are dropped; the rest are stored on ArticleScore (entities[], event_type, matches[]).

Where the matches go: into the event-impact lookup (how this holding moved after past events of that type), into the direction forecast as features, and into the morning brief ("3 stories touch your holdings today", each with its holding and the measured past effect). Code produces every number; news alone never raises a red alert (§6.3).

19.4 The models (F3 to F5, MA-121 to MA-123)

ModelAnswersMethodTrust
Market moodCalm and rising, choppy, stressed or falling, with its probabilityA regime model over cross-asset features, smoothed so it does not flip dailyGood
RiskFour-week volatility and the chance of a fall over 5%, per holdingA volatility model and gradient-boosted trees, combined and calibratedGood
Similar daysWhat followed the 30 days since 2015 most like today, after 5, 20 and 60 daysNearest neighbours over a feature vector, the last 20 days left outGood, and easy to explain
Event impactsHow this holding moved after past rate surprises, inflation surprises, elections, conflict, tariffs, pandemics, earnings misses, OPEC+ decisionsEvent studies: average move, spread, number of cases, confidence interval; under 8 cases marked low confidenceFair
DirectionThe chance of being up in four weeks, the likely range (10th, 50th, 90th percentile) and the main driversGradient-boosted trees combined with the four above, calibrated; conformal intervals for the rangeModest

Claude's part is the same as everywhere (§1): it files each day's news under the event types (Haiku, through Batches) and explains the result. Every number, the event impacts included, comes from code.

19.5 The strategy layer: where the goal is won (F7, MA-125)

Forecasts become suggestions about the money the household already moves: contributions always continue; new money tilts away from holdings in a stressed or falling mood or with a high chance of a deep fall; a trend filter; rebalancing within bands; and a drawdown budget the owner sets. It never says sell everything, stays ISA-safe, and only suggests (§1). It shows as the suggested split on the contribution's check-in (§4.9), beside the rules engine's.

19.6 How it proves itself (F2, MA-120)

  • A backtest lab replays any strategy one day at a time over the ten years, with fees, next-day pricing for OEICs and monthly contributions, and reports yearly growth, the worst fall, the worst year, time below a previous high and the calls' calibration, against buy-and-hold with monthly contributions and a simple trend rule. Its first report is the rules engine itself, years before its live track record could say as much.
  • Walk-forward tests train on the past and test on the years after, with a gap so no window overlaps; the last two years are held back for a final test. A model that does not beat the base rate there is not used.
  • Practice first. Every forecast is a Forecast row, graded when its four weeks are up. For each holding the Forecaster appears in weekday cards only after 8 live weeks beating both the base rate and the rules engine; until then it appears only in the Saturday review, labelled "in practice".
  • It switches itself off when its live record is worse than the base rate for four weeks, and the owner is told. Models are retrained monthly; each version is a ModelVersion row with its test scores.

When the two engines disagree, the card says so; below the confidence floor it says "no clear view". On the dashboard, a Forecast screen per holding: a fan chart of the likely range, the mood timeline, the similar days drawn over today's chart, the event-impact table, the main drivers, candles for ETFs and shares, and both engines' track records side by side. Both are designed first (design-first), with privacy mode and both themes.

19.8 Where it runs and what it costs

The data is small (a few thousand days for a few dozen series), so daily forecasts run in a Lambda from the same image, and the monthly retrain runs in a Lambda too (up to 15 minutes and 10 GB), moving to a Fargate task only if it outgrows that; the one-off GDELT backfill runs as such a task. Models are files in S3 behind their ModelVersion rows. The data is free; EODHD Historian (£19.99 a month) only if a free source fails and the admin approves (§19.2), inside the £50 line for AWS and others; compute is a few dollars a month; Haiku filing the news is well under a dollar a month, inside the Claude ceiling of §15.1.

19.9 Order

F1 (data) and F2 (the lab) can start any time after phase 10, and F2's first report tests the rules engine. F3 and F4 follow, F5 after them, F6 once a holding's market view and the track record exist (phases 12 and 13), F7 after F2 and F3. F2, F3 and F7 do most of the work for the goal; F5 is the least reliable piece. The bounds of §4.5 hold: this is software for one household; sharing its calls with other households would start to look like regulated advice.
