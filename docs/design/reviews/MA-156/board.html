The open point for this round

With privacy on, does the card hide the rate too?

On the planned board 6b, privacy hid both amounts but kept the rate (₹113.57 per £), and your own messages, which Telegram draws, still show what you typed. Rate plus one amount gives the other, so the hidden figure could be worked out. Each option settles it its own way, and the privacy rule can travel: any option’s rule can go with any flow.

1aHide what is yours

A rate worked out from your own figures is hidden; the ECB rate is public, so it stays with its date.

1bNo figure on the card

Amounts, units and every rate go. Only words, currencies and dates stay. Show amounts reveals one card.

1cTidy your message away

The bot deletes your message with the amount once read, so nothing on screen pairs with the rate, which stays.

1a

Four ways on

the planned flow, sharpened

The bot stops, says why in plain words and asks how to record the money: the pounds that arrived, the ECB rate, a rupee holding, or cancel. Each way leads to its own Confirm card.

Privacy: hide what is yours, keep what is public. Amounts and units go. A rate worked out from your own figures goes too. The ECB rate is published for everyone, so it stays with its date.

The correction card

  • Saves nothing, and says why first: Balanced is held in pounds (GBP). The last line says nothing is saved yet.
  • Four ways on, a button each. A rupee holding shows only when there is one.
  • Why pounds? opens in place: “Pounds (GBP), from the factsheet you sent on 3 March.”

The Confirm card, two currencies

  • The card edits in place into one question; you type 176.10 or send the platform’s screenshot.
  • Your figure leads: £176.10 in the headline; the rupees and the rate they imply, ₹113.57 per £, in a quieter line.
  • Saved in pounds, with ₹20,000 as the original and the source “as credited”.

Privacy on: your rate is hidden

  • Both amounts and the units are hidden. ₹113.57 per £ comes from your two figures, so it goes too, and the card says why.
  • Show amounts shows them on this card only, as Show pounds does in the Telegram kit.
  • Your own messages still show what you typed: Telegram draws them.

Privacy on: the ECB rate stays

  • The ECB rate stays, with its date: anyone can look up ₹112.50 per £ for Fri 23 Oct.
  • The trade-off: with your ₹20,000 in view, the public rate gives the pounds away. A card seen on its own does not.

For

  • Closest to the planned boards; the least to redraw.
  • Every way on is a button, so nothing is hidden behind typing.
  • Works unchanged before MA-44: the estimate button is left out.

Against

  • One more card and one more tap than 1b.
  • A new kind of card beside the guided flow’s own questions.
  • Privacy depends on the source: a reader has to know which rates are public.
1b

Ask again, in pounds

Recommended

The correction is the guided flow’s amount question, asked again in the holding’s currency: “How many pounds arrived?” You type them or send the platform’s screenshot. The ECB estimate is one tap, with its figure on the button. A rupee holding is named when there is one. Then Confirm.

Privacy: no amount and no rate on the card. With privacy on, a money card shows only words, currencies and dates. Amounts, units and rates all go, and the estimate button loses its figure. Show amounts shows them on that card only.

The question, in pounds

  • Says why, then asks: Balanced is held in pounds, so it can’t take rupees. How many pounds arrived?
  • Four ways on: type the pounds (or send the screenshot), the estimate, the rupee holding by name, or Cancel. With two or more rupee holdings the button reads A rupee holding and lists them.
  • The estimate shows its figure before you choose it: about £177.78, worked out by the engine at the ECB rate of Fri 23 Oct.

The Confirm card, two currencies

  • The question card drops its buttons once you answer, so no old button can be tapped.
  • Your figure leads: £176.10. From ₹20,000, so ₹113.57 per £, in the quieter line, worked out by the engine.
  • Technical details holds the stored fields (1b·5).

The ECB estimate

  • Estimate is the first word. The card edits in place from the question; Edit goes back to it.
  • The source and its date are named, and what makes it firm: a statement, a screenshot or the next valuation.
  • Before the daily rates load (MA-44) the estimate button is left out; nothing else changes.

Privacy on: the question

  • No figure on the card: the rupees are masked, the ECB rate is named by its date, and the button reads Use the ECB estimate.
  • The correction still reads: the currencies, the reason and the four ways on stay.

Privacy on: Confirm

  • Amounts, units and the rate are hidden: nothing on the card leads to a figure.
  • Show amounts shows them on this card only.
  • Your own messages still show what you typed. The tidy-away of 1c can be added if you want the screen clean too.

Technical mode

  • The fold, open: what is stored, in plain labels: recorded 176.10 GBP, typed 20,000 INR, the rate, the source “as credited” and its date.
  • The summary stays first; simple mode keeps the fold shut, as in 1b·2.

Why it is recommended

  • One pattern: it is the guided flow’s own “How much, in pounds?” (ARCHITECTURE §9.2), asked again in the holding’s currency.
  • The exact figure leads; the estimate is one tap with its figure in view; Confirm stays a separate, deliberate tap.
  • Works unchanged before MA-44, and privacy is one rule that is simple to build and to test.

Against

  • Typing the pounds is a way on without a button; the card has to say so.
  • Privacy hides even the public ECB rate.
  • Your own messages still show what you typed.
1c

Correct inside Confirm

one card

No question first: the bot goes straight to the Confirm card, with the correction as its first line and the ECB estimate filled in, the recorded pounds and the rupees you typed one under the other. Exact pounds, a rupee holding and Cancel are buttons; typing the pounds replaces the estimate.

Privacy: tidy your message away, keep the rate. With privacy on, the bot deletes your message with the amount once it is read (a bot may delete a member’s messages in its private chat within 48 hours), and the card says so. With nothing on screen to pair it with, the rate stays.

One card

  • The mismatch, right before Confirm, as the story’s title puts it. Recorded and You typed sit one under the other.
  • Fewest taps: Confirm the estimate, then the second check.
  • The risk: the estimate is the default, so a reflex Confirm saves an estimate where the exact pounds were known.

Exact pounds

  • Typing the pounds replaces the estimate: the first card drops its buttons and a new Confirm card follows your figure.
  • Before the daily rates load (MA-44) there is no estimate to fill in, so the first card has to ask for the pounds, as 1b does: two designs to build.

Privacy on: your message tidied away

  • Your message is gone from the chat, and the card’s first line says why. The record and the audit row keep it.
  • The rate stays: nothing left on screen pairs with it.
  • The trade-off: the bot changes your side of the chat, which is new in MyPaisa; the message goes from your other devices too.

For

  • Fewest taps, and the mismatch sits right before Confirm.
  • Both currencies read at once, at the same weight.
  • The only option where nothing on screen gives the amount away.

Against

  • The estimate is the default, so a reflex Confirm records it.
  • Before MA-44 it needs a second design, the 1b question.
  • Deleting your messages is new behaviour, and it reaches every device.
