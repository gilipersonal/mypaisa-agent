Signing in on the web, three ways

You asked for every designed web page on stubbed data, including login page, with an options round for each planned area before it is built. Sign-in is the first. Its boards today are planned, not picked, and they are three different things: a navy split page for signing in, a specification sheet for the refusals, and the join page you picked on MA-301.

Below are three ways to make them one. Each is drawn for the same six moments, on a laptop and on a phone, in light and dark: signing in, an account linked to nobody, a paused member, the passkey offer, an invite link, and waiting, offline and no answer. The words are the same in all three, so what differs is the page. Nothing is built and no picked board has changed.

Pick one, or say “none of these” and what to change. The first tab is the one I would pick, with the reason.

1b · The navy door. The window in two halves, as today’s planned sign-in page. Trade-off: Half the window repeats one line at every sign-in. On a phone the navy pushes the buttons into the lower half. The join page you picked changes shape, though not its words. A paused member’s one line sits beside an empty navy half.

1c · A sheet over the app. The app is already there as bones: the sidebar with the mark, the page as blank blocks, no figure and no name. Signing in is a sheet over it, the size and corner of the chat’s sheet. When you are in, the sheet drops and the blocks fill; the passkey offer shows Home behind it. The same sheet could later serve signing in again when a session ends, and the second check.

Trade-off: Blank blocks before sign-in can read as a page that failed to load, and a refused or paused person is left looking at an app they cannot open. The shell must draw with no session, which the stub does not need. It is the largest build.
