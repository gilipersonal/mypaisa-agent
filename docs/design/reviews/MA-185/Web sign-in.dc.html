MA-185MA-185 · Web sign-in

In a browser a member signs in to the web app with Apple, Google or a passkey. Today’s boards for it are planned, not picked, and they are three different things: a navy split page for signing in (Web v4 · Shell and chat), a specification sheet inside the app’s shell for the refusals (Web v4 · Login, invite and responsive, section 1), and the join page picked on MA-301 (e 1a, a card). Nothing draws a refusal, the passkey offer after a sign-in, or offline as a page a person would meet.

Three options follow. Each draws the same six moments on a laptop (1440) and on a phone (390), in light and dark: signing in; an account linked to nobody; a paused member; the passkey offer; an invite link, new and used; and waiting, offline and no answer. The words are the same in all three, so what differs is the page: where the way in sits, what leads, and how a refusal and an invite read.

The window is two halves. The navy half says what this is: the household’s line when you sign in, the invitation itself when you open an invite link. The other half is the way in. It is today’s planned sign-in page, carried to every moment.

What it gives up: Half the window says the same line at every sign-in, to a member who has read it. On a phone the navy band pushes the buttons into the lower half. The join page picked on MA-301 changes shape, though not its words. A paused member’s one line sits beside an empty navy half.

1cA sheet over the appMA-185 · ARCHITECTURE §4.5, §8.4

The app is already there, as bones: the sidebar with the mark, the page as blank blocks, no figure and no name. Signing in is a sheet over it, the sheet the chat opens in (MA-355 a 1a). When you are in, the sheet drops and the blocks fill.

What it is best at: The shortest way into Home: nothing navigates, the page fills in behind the sheet (the passkey offer shows it). One sheet could serve three moments: signing in, signing in again when a session ends, and the second check before an admin action (MA-76; neither is drawn here). The mark is already where it will stay.

What it gives up: Blank blocks before sign-in can read as a page that failed to load. A refused or paused person is left looking at an app they cannot open. The shell must draw with no session. A sheet that cannot be closed is a new kind of sheet, so it has no grabber and no close button.

If picked, it changes: The web shell learns to draw signed out, and gains a sign-in sheet beside the chat’s. Web v4 · Login, invite and responsive: sections 1 and 3 are both redrawn. Web v4 · Shell and chat gains the signed-out shell and loses Browser · Sign in. It is the largest build of the three.

How it lands: The sheet drops, the dim lifts, the sidebar’s rows and the page’s blocks fill, and the mark arrives. The dim covers the page and not the sidebar, as the chat sheet’s does (MA-360). On a phone the sheet rises from the foot, as the header’s menus do (MA-355 b 1a).
