How it answers
PointerHovering a segment or its row lifts the segment 3 px, dims the others to 40% and puts the name, share and amount in the centre and on the row. A click keeps it selected; a second click on the selected row opens that kind in Assets or the loan in Debts. On the line a hairline follows the pointer and snaps to the month, and the card’s headline becomes the readout: the month, both figures, and ▲ ahead or ▼ behind. It returns to the takeaway when the pointer leaves.
TouchA tap on a segment or a row selects it and it stays selected (a light haptic); a tap on the centre or outside clears it. Every row is 44 pt tall and is the target for a segment too thin to hit. A touch held on the line for 0.15 s, or a sideways drag begun on it, scrubs; an up or down drag scrolls the page. The readout is the headline above the chart, so the finger never covers it; lifting the finger brings the takeaway back. The horizon chips are a segmented control.
KeyboardTab reaches each chart once. In a ring or a bar list ↑ and ↓ move through its rows, Enter opens the selected row, Esc clears. On the line ← and → move one month, Shift with an arrow one year, Home goes to today and End to the horizon’s end. The chips take ← and →. A 3 px focus ring in --mp-focus shows where you are.
Screen readerEach chart has one spoken summary (its takeaway first) and then its rows as a list of buttons: “Property, 74.1 percent, £512,778, 1 of 5”. The line is a slider that reads “June 2028: invested £226,195, owed £221,586”, with an audio graph on iOS and “Year by year” as a table at both doors. Nothing is told by colour alone: every part has its name beside it, the owed line is dashed, and ▲ and ▼ mark ahead and behind.
Privacy modeEvery pound and rupee is masked, in the centre, the rows, the readout, the axis and the spoken labels (“Hidden”). Shares, dates and shapes stay, so the picture still reads beside someone else.
MotionA ring draws in clockwise once and a line draws in under a mask, their dots and labels landing after; a segment answers a touch with the snappy spring; a chip cross-fades the chart and rolls only the digits that change. Under Reduce Motion everything lands in its final state.

How it answers
PointerHovering a segment or its row lifts the segment 3 px, dims the others to 40% and puts the name, share and amount in the centre and on the row. A click keeps it selected; a second click on the selected row opens that kind in Assets or the loan in Debts. On the line a hairline follows the pointer and snaps to the month, and the card’s headline becomes the readout: the month, both figures, and ▲ ahead or ▼ behind. It returns to the takeaway when the pointer leaves.
TouchA tap on a segment or a row selects it and it stays selected (a light haptic); a tap on the centre or outside clears it. Every row is 44 pt tall and is the target for a segment too thin to hit. A touch held on the line for 0.15 s, or a sideways drag begun on it, scrubs; an up or down drag scrolls the page. The readout is the headline above the chart, so the finger never covers it; lifting the finger brings the takeaway back. The horizon chips are a segmented control.
KeyboardTab reaches each chart once. In a ring or a bar list ↑ and ↓ move through its rows, Enter opens the selected row, Esc clears. On the line ← and → move one month, Shift with an arrow one year, Home goes to today and End to the horizon’s end. The chips take ← and →. A 3 px focus ring in --mp-focus shows where you are.
Screen readerEach chart has one spoken summary (its takeaway first) and then its rows as a list of buttons: “Property, 74.1 percent, £512,778, 1 of 5”. The line is a slider that reads “June 2028: invested £226,195, owed £221,586”, with an audio graph on iOS and “Year by year” as a table at both doors. Nothing is told by colour alone: every part has its name beside it, the owed line is dashed, and ▲ and ▼ mark ahead and behind.
Privacy modeEvery pound and rupee is masked, in the centre, the rows, the readout, the axis and the spoken labels (“Hidden”). Shares, dates and shapes stay, so the picture still reads beside someone else.
MotionA ring draws in clockwise once and a line draws in under a mask, their dots and labels landing after; a segment answers a touch with the snappy spring; a chip cross-fades the chart and rolls only the digits that change. Under Reduce Motion everything lands in its final state.
