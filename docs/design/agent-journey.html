MyPaisa Agent Journey

How a household uses MyPaisa Agent, one flow at a time: what the member does, the path it takes through Telegram, AWS and Claude, what the member sees, and what keeps it safe. The UK holdings come first; the household's Indian funds, shares and ETFs join them in rupees, with pounds beside every figure. Each flow names the Jira stories that build it.

A Telegram bot and a read-only web app AWS us-east-1, dev and prod Claude through the Anthropic API UK and Indian holdings, GBP and INR It never trades

Every figure, rate and fund name on this page is illustrative. "You" are the household's owner; Asha is the second member. The facts come from ARCHITECTURE.md (India in §4.7, §5.3 and §22), PLAN.md (phase 13 for India) and the MA-101 and MA-102 design decisions.

Four rules every flow keeps

From ARCHITECTURE.md §1. Each path below shows where they bite.

Rule 1

Claude never produces a number

Every figure comes from code with tests. Claude chooses what to say and how to say it.

Rule 2

The bot cannot trade

No broker login, no orders. It suggests only actions that suit the account: ISA-safe for the UK wrappers, wrapper-aware for Indian accounts.

Rule 3

Summary first, detail on tap

Every message has a plain top and a technical underside. Nothing technical is shown unasked.

Rule 4

One write path

Every change to the household's records, whatever the screen, goes through the bot: a card, a Confirm tap, an audit row. The web app is read-only for those records. The one exception is the owner's Admin center, which acts on admin settings and operations, never on a record, each behind a fresh check and an audit row ("Dashboard will be read only (except for admin)").

How to read a flow

Each path is a numbered sequence across five lanes. An arrow is a hand-off from one lane to another; a box is work done inside one lane. Safeguards are underlined in coral. On a narrow screen the same steps stack, each tagged with its lanes.

Member

You or Asha, on a phone in Telegram or on a laptop in a browser.

Telegram

The Bot API, the chat with its buttons, the Mini App container, the Login Widget, Face ID through Telegram's prompt.

AWS

API Gateway, the webhook λ, the SQS FIFO updates queue, the agent λ, the SQS work queue and worker λ, the SQS outbox and sender λ, DynamoDB, S3, Cognito, EventBridge Scheduler, Step Functions (phase 12), SNS, CloudWatch.

Claude

The Anthropic API: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5, server-side web search and fetch, Message Batches.

External data

Free sources first: yfinance, Twelve Data's free tier, ECB FX, issuer pages, FT, RSS. For India (phase 13): AMFI's NAV file, mfapi.in, yfinance .NS and .BO, NSE and BSE bhavcopy, the RBI/FBIL rate, Indian news feeds. Google, when it vouches for a sign-in.

The safeguards, in one place

They recur across the flows, so here is what each one means.

Confirm before any writeEvery change is shown as a card first and saved only after the member taps Confirm. A change across several records is one plan with one Confirm.
The audit rowAn AuditEvent in the same DynamoDB transaction as the change: who, when, through what, before and after, which run. Hash-chained, never deleted.
Privacy mode/privacy hides pounds and rupees in every card, in the Mini App and on the web; on the currency check's cards (MA-156, ARCHITECTURE §4.7) it shows no amount and no rate, only words, currencies and dates.
The currency checkEvery holding keeps the one currency it is bought and valued in. An amount typed in another is never saved as it is: the bot says why and asks for the amount that arrived (MA-156).
The second checkFace ID or a 6-digit PIN on the phone after 12 hours idle and after Confirm on any change; a fresh passkey or Google check in the browser for the owner's actions.
The spend ladderThe month's Claude spend is read before every call; at 70, 85, 95 and 100% of the ceiling the bot steps down. Alerts are never dropped.
Retries and dead-letter queuesEvery hand-off goes through SQS: retried with backoff, idempotent, and kept in a dead-letter queue with an alarm and a replay when it keeps failing.

Flows 1 to 12 follow the UK holdings. Flows 13 to 16 bring India into the picture: money in any currency (phase 1), then the Indian portfolio, the two-currency household and India's place in the day (phase 13).

Signing in

There are no passwords. Inside Telegram, Telegram vouches for the member. On a laptop it is Telegram, Google or a passkey. Every route goes through the environment's Cognito user pool and ends in the same short-lived session, and a change needs a second check on the phone.

The paths

Route 1: the Mini App, inside TelegramTelegram's signed initData, exchanged for Cognito tokens

MemberTelegramAWSClaudeExternal data
  1. Asha taps the web_app button on the Portfolio card
  2. Telegram opens console.mypaisapp.com from CloudFront and S3 (a public shell with no data) and hands it initData: an HMAC over the user and a timestamp, keyed by the bot token
  3. The page sends initData to API Gateway /auth/telegram. Cognito's custom-auth Lambdas check the signature in constant time, an age under five minutes, and no replay
  4. Cognito issues Asha's tokens. api λ keeps them behind a session cookie for its own host: HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, a day inside Telegram. Access tokens last five minutes
  5. Second check: after 12 hours idle, a lock screen with no figures. Face ID through Telegram's prompt, or the 6-digit PIN
  6. Asha passes Face ID. Three wrong PINs keep it locked, changes included, for 15 minutes and tell the owner
  7. On every /v1 call the authorizer checks the session, Asha's status and her session epoch; the data routes are GET only

Route 2: a browser on a laptopThe Telegram Login Widget, Sign in with Google, or a passkey

MemberTelegramAWSClaudeExternal data
  1. Asha opens console.mypaisapp.com; CloudFront serves the shell and the sign-in page
  2. Telegram: the Login Widget, one tap, confirmed in the Telegram app on her phone
  3. The signed payload reaches the same custom-auth Lambdas: HMAC keyed by the bot token, five-minute freshness, no replay
  4. Google: Sign in with Google through auth.mypaisapp.com, Cognito's Google provider
  5. Cognito maps Google's stable account id (sub) to the member it was linked to, never the address. A Google account linked to nobody is refused

Five minutes, refreshed by the api, never held by page scripts.

Every call

The 07:15 morning brief, end to end

At 07:00 the prices come in from free sources; by 07:10 the engine has scored every holding; at 07:15 each member's brief arrives. If a price is late, the brief still goes, and says so.

Trigger EventBridge Scheduler, Europe/London, each weekdayPhase 2 (prices, engine)Phase 3 (the brief)

The morning, by the clock

07:00Ingest. The Scheduler queues one job per holding; free sources first.
07:10Signals. The engine has scored every holding; stances stay in shadow until phase 8.
07:15Brief. Once for the household and once for each member who wants one, in their own preferences.
07:45Dead man's switch. If the brief has not gone out, an alarm tells the owner.

The path

MemberTelegramAWSClaudeExternal data
  1. 07:00 EventBridge Scheduler. The /pause flag is checked first, then one ingest job per holding goes on the SQS work queue
  2. worker λ runs each job down the holding's chain: yfinance, then Twelve Data's free tier, then the issuer page; ECB for FX
  3. Bars in the native currency with their source; pence turned into pounds; raw answers kept in S3
  4. A source that answers nothing is skipped. Last in every chain is stale: the previous close, labelled, never silently reused, and left out of scoring
  5. The finished job queues the next. The engine runs its self-tests, then indicators, pillars, macro and the stance, and writes Signals to DynamoDB by 07:10
  6. 07:15 a brief job per member reads the signals and that member's preferences: privacy mode, simple or technical, their accounts and a line per household member
  7. The engine's numbers and the cached card rubric go to Sonnet, after the spend meter is read
  8. Claude writes the words. A validator checks every figure against the engine's; on a refusal or an error the plain template goes instead
  9. The card and its PNG chart go on the SQS outbox, one lane per chat, so they arrive in order; sender λ honours Telegram's limits and retry_after
  10. Telegram delivers the brief
  11. You read the top; the technical detail is one tap away
  12. 07:45 CloudWatch: if the brief has not gone out, the dead man's switch alarm reaches the owner
  13. From phase 12 one Step Functions state machine drives the same jobs (ingest, engine, Forecaster, grade, calibrate, brief) with retries and backoff on every step, and the ops warden repairs what it safely can.
  14. From phase 13 the brief also carries India's previous day. NSE and BSE close at 15:30 IST and AMFI's fund prices arrive by the UK evening, so the Indian jobs have run long before 07:00; flow 16 follows them.

When something fails

Every hand-off goes through an SQS queue. A bad minute at Telegram, at the Anthropic API or at a data source costs a delay, never a message or a day's work. What keeps failing waits in a dead-letter queue until someone, or later the ops warden, replays it.

The path

MemberTelegramAWSClaudeExternal data
  1. worker λ's ingest job for one holding calls a source, which times out
  2. The message returns to the SQS work queue after a growing wait: five tries with backoff. Meanwhile the chain falls through, and the brief goes out labelled stale
  3. After the fifth try it moves to the dead-letter queue: kept 14 days, with a copy in S3 under raw/dlq/ for good
  4. A CloudWatch alarm fires the moment the dead-letter queue holds anything; the alert router sends failed work
  5. Your Telegram chat and the Admin center's Alerts page show it, with [Replay]
  6. You tap Replay once the cause is fixed
  7. The messages move back to their queue. Handlers are idempotent, keyed by update id, message id, or job id and date, so nothing runs twice
  8. Replay, by phase. Phase 1: scripts/redrive.sh <queue>. Phase 5: the [Replay] button on the alert. Phase 12: the ops warden replays on its own once the cause is healthy, at most three times per message with growing gaps; failing the same way again opens an incident.
  9. A member's update that fails three times within about a minute goes to the updates dead-letter queue, and the member is told it was kept
  10. Replayed after more than an hour, an update is answered still want this? rather than acted on
  11. A Confirm tap whose PendingAction has expired is refused, so an old tap never saves a change by surprise

Never silentAn alarm the moment a dead-letter queue holds anything; the brief, the wrap and alerts are always attempted.
Old taps are not trustedOver an hour old: "still want this?". An expired Confirm: refused.

  1. After the 15:30 IST close on a trading day (from NSE's holiday calendar), EventBridge Scheduler (Europe/London) checks the /pause flag, then puts one ingest job per Indian share or ETF on the SQS work queue
  2. worker λ runs the in_listed chain: yfinance .NS, then .BO, then the exchanges' bhavcopy end-of-day file
  3. Daily candles in rupees, with their source; raw answers kept in S3
  4. In the UK evening, one job per Indian fund: AMFI's daily NAV file, matched by scheme code and ISIN, with mfapi.in behind it
  5. One NAV a day per scheme, no candles, like a UK OEIC
  6. The ECB reference rate includes INR, with the RBI/FBIL rate behind it: each Indian Valuation is stored in rupees and in pounds on the day
  7. News jobs read Economic Times, Mint and Business Standard by RSS, NSE company announcements and GDELT's India coverage
  8. Haiku tags each story through Batches: the companies, sectors and central bank it names, its event type
  9. The tags. Code matches each story to the holdings through exposure maps from the fund houses' monthly disclosures
  10. 07:10 the engine scores every holding. For Indian holdings the macro pillar reads the India basket: the Nifty 50 against Indian government bonds, India VIX, the rupee against the dollar, foreign portfolio investor flows and the RBI's policy rate
  11. 07:15 the brief, India's previous day included, through the outbox and sender λ
  12. A source that answers nothing is skipped and the chain falls through to stale: labelled, left out of scoring, as in flow 5. The calendars add the RBI's policy dates, India's inflation releases, NSE holidays and results season. The Forecaster (phase 11) takes the Indian holdings on the same terms: nothing is shown until its gates are passed.

Safeguards on this path

One clockEvery schedule in Europe/London; the Indian jobs run after the 15:30 IST close and after AMFI's NAVs.
Free sources firstAMFI, mfapi.in, yfinance, the bhavcopy. A paid feed (Twelve Data's Grow plan, Zerodha Kite Connect) only on the admin's approval, inside the £50 cap.
Stale is labelledEach Indian chain falls through to stale: the previous close, labelled and kept out of scoring.
The exchange's own calendarNSE's trading holidays come from the exchange's calendar.
Retries and dead-letter queuesEach Indian job is a work-queue job: five tries with backoff, then its dead-letter queue and an alarm.
Numbers from codeHaiku tags the stories; code matches and scores them. News alone never raises a red alert.
